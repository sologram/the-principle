# The Second Axiom of Intention

## Text

**Second Axiom: Things tend toward completeness.**

> **In a system with variation and selection, incomplete structures tend to be replaced, corrected, and pruned; relatively complete structures tend to be retained, copied, and propagated.**

**Everyday anchor: mending the sheep pen after the sheep are lost.** The sheep are gone — boundary leakage is exposed; mend the pen — adjust the boundary, that is, patch the loophole; the sheep stop getting out — tending toward completeness. The isomorphic statement at the level of individual learning is "you get burned once and you grow twice as wise": once erroneous existence is exposed, the boundary is redrawn. This is the naive semantic intuition behind "things tend toward completeness," and it does not depart from ordinary human experience.

**Position of the axiom.** The Second Axiom is the **dynamic criterion** of meta-science, the third link of the syllogism:

> Zeroth Axiom (**existence entrance**: subject terms, boundaries) → First Axiom (**correctness adjudication**: completeness) → Second Axiom (**direction of approach**: dynamics).

The Zeroth Axiom answers "what is there," the First Axiom answers "what is right," the Second Axiom answers "which way it goes." Without the Second Axiom the system is only a static set of axioms; with it, the system begins to move and can explain evolution, progress, learning, and selection. The outline names it the Axiom of Intention: **the content name is "things tend toward completeness," the dynamics name is "intention" (directional trend)** — the non-teleological reading of "intention" is in Note 1.

**Core meaning: "tend toward" is not "already are."** This axiom does not claim that things are already complete — "reality is always imperfect" (outline Q&A); it claims that things exhibit **a tendency to converge toward completeness** in their evolution. Analogy: "gravity makes objects fall" does not mean "all objects have already landed"; "life tends to adapt" does not mean "life is already perfectly adapted."

Note that this is a statement of statistical tendency: it says which kind of state persists, not the rate of increase. Case-by-case adjudication of completeness is bivalent (First Axiom: complete within the boundary is correct); the trend is diachronic — a single trajectory need not be monotone, and local regression or stagnation can occur (such as the dark ages of knowledge), but the tendency to occupy high-completeness states grows over time.

**Conditions of applicability and scale-freeness.** The tendency toward completeness holds in any system satisfying the following three conditions:

| Condition | Description |
|---|---|
| Reproducibility | The state can be copied / transmitted |
| Mutability | Copying can produce variation |
| Selectivity | Different states have different probabilities of persistence |

Genes, memes, technologies, institutions, algorithms, scientific theories, and markets are all on the list. **The axiom is scale-free**: the trend contains no scale parameter and applies at every scale and in every expressive space (matter's weak expression and mind's strong expression alike).

**Source of generalization: Darwinism.** Abstracting the three elements of variation, selection, and heredity away from the biological carrier yields the formula above. Natural selection is merely one instantiation mechanism of approaching completeness. The definition of fitness is given by the First Axiom:

> **Fitness = everything required for the judgment relative to the environmental problem (boundary B) has been expressed = relative completeness.**

"Relative completeness" means: with respect to the specified boundary B, what the judgment requires has been expressed (the completeness criterion of the First Axiom, bivalent case by case); comparison across boundaries is an engineering-layer ordering (see the Pruning theorem) and does not enter the definition layer.

**The closed loop with the First Axiom.**

> The First Axiom defines completeness = correctness; the Second Axiom describes things tending toward completeness; therefore the things observed tend to be correct.

The First Axiom makes "correct" a criterion; this axiom supplies the dynamic process: incomplete structures are eliminated, complete structures persist — so the world presents a picture of "tending toward correctness." **The unity of criterion and trend**: the former gives the static standard, the latter gives the diachronic direction.

**The mechanism chain.**

> Incomplete → information blockage (Games theorem: turbulence originates from information blockage) → turbulence → selection → tending toward completeness.

Turbulence is not noise; it is the source of selection pressure. Without turbulence, selection stalls. Information blockage persists in games, and turbulence forces the boundary to be redrawn — Axioms 1 through 3 are stitched into a feedback loop at this seam.

**Darwinian boundary principle and the Pruning theorem.**

> **Darwinian boundary principle: adjusting a thing's boundary is an effective means of evolving toward completeness.**

"Reality is always imperfect — so reality must be carved to find completeness" (outline Q&A): completeness is discovered by carving boundaries, not merely by waiting for selection. The "mending" of "mending the sheep pen," the "patching the loopholes" of the First Axiom, and the "adjusting boundaries" of Darwin are three names for the same engineering operation.

**Pruning theorem**: efficient exploratory engineering must prune: incomplete "correctness," non-ignorant "freedom," non-efficient "justice," "meaning" without a price, and purposeless "evolution." Pruning belongs to the **engineering layer** — the Principle is the courthouse, and order beyond the courthouse still requires dispatching large numbers of police officers, or even armies; the Principle adjudicates, engineering prunes. This axiom supplies the direction: since things tend toward completeness, pruning the incomplete parts accords with the direction of evolution. The guard against degeneration is unchanged: prune by topic, not by difficulty (difficulty is boundary confusion); freezing pruning violates this axiom.

**Self-reference and scale-freeness.** This axiom refers to itself and applies to itself:

> **Scientific layer**: things tend toward completeness (the content does not change, by the scientific eternity law). **Textual layer**: the text of the Principle is a thing, so the text tends toward completeness — if something better appears, absorb it (outline Q&A).

Mutable self-reference is forbidden: what evolves is the boundary and the textual expression, not the definition of "completeness" (the definition belongs to the First Axiom, see the Self-reference theorem). "Scientific principles must not change" does not conflict with "if something better appears, absorb it": this is the relationship between "science" and "the human text of science."

**Table of exemplars.**

| Succession | Form of incompleteness | Boundary action |
|---|---|---|
| Geocentrism → heliocentrism | Accumulation in this round, patch-style leakage | Cut and redraw |
| Newtonian mechanics → relativity / quantum mechanics | Failure in the high-speed and microscopic domains (leakage outside the domain of applicability) | Redraw the domain of applicability |
| Phlogiston → oxidation theory | An ad hoc patch that adds weight | Redraw the conceptual boundary |
| Falsification event | A breach in the theory's boundary is exposed | Pruning (engineering layer), the boundary drawn more precisely |

Falsification does not mean "completely wrong": Newtonian mechanics is still relatively complete in the low-speed macroscopic domain. Falsification is the **mechanism for selecting incomplete theories**, belonging to engineering-layer pruning and not to the Principle's adjudication — love-of-wisdom boundary law: the passage from uncertainty to certainty is the boundary between philosophy and science, and falsification is the making-certain of the boundary.

**Summary.** The Second Axiom does three things:

1. **It injects dynamics**: a static system of axioms becomes a dynamic framework of evolution, able to explain change, progress, and selection;
2. **It abstracts Darwinism**: the general logic of variation–selection–heredity holds across the four quadrants of nature, society, form, and meta-science;
3. **It connects criterion and trend**: the First Axiom defines correctness, the Second Axiom has things move toward it — criterion and trend unified.

## Notes

### 1. Intention: a directional trend without purpose

"Intention" is the dynamics name, not a teleological assertion:

- A living organism "intends" to survive and reproduce — this requires only selection pressure, not consciousness;
- A scientific theory "intends" to explain more phenomena — this requires only a selection mechanism, not teleology;
- A market "intends" to clear — this requires only a price mechanism, not central planning.

> **A system with no consciousness, no purpose, and no designer exhibits a directional tendency toward the better through selection pressure; the macroscopic effect is equivalent to "intention."**

"Purpose" is liberated from metaphysics and restored as a product of selection dynamics. It meets the Pruning theorem: pruning cuts "**purposeless evolution**" — purposeless = a stream of variation without a direction toward completeness (noise), which is exactly what pruning targets. Hence "intention" is not a metaphorical disclaimer but the meeting of two words in the same theorem.

### 2. Formalization: the trend is not a measure

Completeness (First Axiom) is a **closing condition** (bivalent within the boundary), not a scalar; "more complete / relatively complete" across boundaries is an **engineering-layer ordering** used for pruning and selection — outline precedent: "game solver = efficiency annotation source" (conjecture section). Any completeness metric function (such as C: Ω → [0,1]) can only be an engineering-layer annotation source and must not enter the definition layer; statements like "the overwhelming majority of initial states" require a measure-theoretic definition and do not count until that is defined. Trustworthy deduction must be based on axioms: a formalization that does not trace back to an axiom is mere notation. The main text of this axiom is itself a trend statement (which kind of state persists), sufficient to bear criterion and direction alike; no measure is needed.

### 3. Freedom and permissiveness: the starting point of tending toward completeness is not freedom

The outline's quadruple: **freedom = correct ignorance; order = correct knowledge; servitude = wrong knowledge; permissiveness = wrong ignorance.** Freedom is in the quadrant of correctness: the ignorance is correctly expressed (which matters are open, who decides — everything the judgment requires is present), and is not a defect. The starting point of tending toward completeness is **permissiveness** (wrong ignorance) and **servitude** (wrong knowledge) — erroneous existence is exposed and the redrawing of boundaries begins. Freedom (correct ignorance) provides the space of variation; permissiveness is the object of pruning. Confusing freedom with permissiveness hangs the error "ignorance is incompleteness" on freedom.

### 4. The repositioning of science history and falsification

The history of science is the most intuitive empirical evidence for this axiom: a paradigm shift is an increase in completeness (see the table of exemplars). Popper's falsificationism is repositioned: **falsification is not an endpoint but a selection mechanism** — and it belongs to engineering-layer pruning. The Principle does not accept falsificationism as a criterion of demarcation (philosophy of science itself no longer upholds it); the positive criterion of demarcation is the commitment/delivery double axis, and falsification's legitimate identity within the system is "the making-certain of boundaries" (love-of-wisdom boundary law).

### 5. Clarifying the entropy analogy

The statistical analogy borrows only the point that "the macroscopic trend is determinate while the microscopic trajectory is arbitrary." The rise of completeness and thermodynamic entropy lie on **different axes**: completeness = closure of adjudication (criterion side), entropy = counting of microstates (thermodynamics); the same system can contain both. The Fourth Axiom's source of generalization is the maximum entropy principle (the entropy of inferred meaning), which does not conflict with this axiom — one is the unbiased baseline of inference, the other is the direction of evolution; each governs its own expressive space.

### 6. Hegel's theorem: the blocked form of tending toward completeness

**The only lesson humanity learns from history is that it learns nothing from history (1).** This is the macroscopic manifestation of a broken delivery chain: the lesson (the precedent) is expressed but not absorbed into the boundary — tending toward completeness at the textual layer is blocked. It does not refute this axiom; it identifies the blocking mechanism: information blockage (Games theorem) can persist on the scale of generations, selection pressure fails, and tending toward completeness stalls. It mirrors this axiom's self-reference: the Principle commits to "if something better appears, absorb it," which is precisely a structural immunity to Hegel's theorem.

### 7. Relations to the other axioms

| Related axiom | Relation |
|---|---|

| Fourth Axiom | Freedom (correct ignorance) provides the space of variation; permissiveness (wrong ignorance) is the starting point of tending toward completeness |

| Sixth Axiom | Price is an information compression mechanism, supplying a distributed annotation for ordering the completeness of alternatives |

### 8. Applications and the conjecture layer

- **An explanation of scientific progress**: a paradigm shift = an increase in completeness (see the table of exemplars, empirical corroboration);
- **A reading of AGI training**: learning = a search for completeness in the hypothesis space — an application-layer reading, supplying a Principle-level footnote to "why does learning work";
- **The direction of alignment**: "align with the Principle, not humans" (**conjecture**) — alignment is not a static matching but making machine intelligence evolve along the direction of tending toward completeness; its behavioral content depends on the unfolding of the Third through Seventh Axioms and is not a direct corollary of this axiom.

### 9. Summary

The Second Axiom is the engine of the system: **things tend toward completeness** — the directionality of variation, selection, and persistence, holding across scales and across expressive spaces. It holds of itself (the textual layer absorbs what is better, the content of the scientific layer does not change), it gives engineering a direction (pruning, patching loopholes, adjusting boundaries), and it gives the world a trend (the things observed tend to be correct). Criterion and trend are unified: the First Axiom says "what is correct," the Second Axiom has things move toward it.
