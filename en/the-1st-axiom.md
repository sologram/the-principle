# The First Axiom of Correctness

## Text

**First Axiom: Correctness is completeness.**

> **Completeness = everything required for the judgment has been expressed.**

**Everyday anchor: correctness admits no loophole.** To say in ordinary speech that an argument "has a loophole" means that there is some place at which the judgment falls through — it cannot cover, cannot block, cannot be put clearly. "Correctness admits no loophole" means that no place is tolerated where the judgment falls through; judgment falling through = what the judgment requires has not been expressed (no verdict, no ground, no test); hence **correct = everything required for the judgment has been expressed = complete**. This is a strictification of everyday semantic intuition, not an arbitrary redefinition: it removes the vagueness from "loophole" and pins it to the places where the judgment falls through.

**Position of the axiom.** The First Axiom is the **correctness-adjudication core** of meta-science, the criterion clause of "correct metaphysics." It stipulates: whether a proposition, system, or behavior is "correct" is not decided by subjective preference, authoritative endorsement, or frequency of experience (subjective/objective = disagreement/agreement), but by whether it is **complete**. The Zeroth Axiom adjudicates "what may enter discussion" (existence, subject terms, boundaries); the First Axiom adjudicates "how correctness is recognized once it has entered discussion."

**Core meaning.** The chain of equivalences, fixed once and for all:

> **Right = correct = complete = everything required for the judgment has been expressed = no undecided matter within the boundary = there is commitment and there is delivery = the criterion lies within the system itself (formal)**

- **Correctness is not "being believed," but admitting no loophole**: nothing omitted, no contradiction, closable. "Must not" is a prohibition, not a description — this is of a kind with the Pruning theorem's "must prune: incomplete 'correctness'," and it settles the character of the definition as a constraint: completeness is not discovered, it is legislated for "correctness." An incomplete proposition, even if temporarily effective, still belongs to erroneous existence.
- **Completeness is not "containing everything," but everything required for the judgment has been expressed.** What the judgment requires refers to the criterion side: criteria, rules, procedures, precedents; the structure being adjudicated belongs to the object side and is admitted by the Zeroth Axiom. The material needed for testing is external input; it need not, and should not, lie within the expression.
- **Correctness and completeness are equivalent.** If a system claims correctness while the elements required to judge it have not all been expressed (there exist parts that are undecidable, uninterpretable, or undeliverable), then its correctness does not hold — it has a loophole.

Within this: **decidable** = there is a verdict; **interpretable** = the criterion is within the boundary; **deliverable** = there is an executable test. "No undecided matter" follows from the three holding, not from an exhaustive enumeration of everything.

**Decision rule.**

> If a system contains within boundary B any part that is undecidable, uninterpretable, or undeliverable, then the system is not correct within B.

Equivalently:

> **Correct = complete within the boundary = everything required for the judgment has been expressed = no loophole.**

This rule **applies case by case; it is not a uniform decision algorithm**: Gödel's limits on proof and Church's limits on algorithms neither limits case-by-case adjudication. Completeness does not claim that everything is provable; it claims only that the criteria are self-sufficient. This is not an empirical generalization but a definitional constraint the system places on "correctness."

**The dual.** Together with the Zeroth Axiom it forms the two halves of completeness of expression:

| Axiom | Side | Completeness |
|---|---|---|
| Zeroth: existence is distinguishable, bounded, expressible | Object side | The structure being adjudicated has been expressed → existence |
| First: completeness = everything required for the judgment has been expressed | Criterion side | The adjudicating apparatus has been expressed → completeness |

**Source of generalization: the Rationality theorem.** "When a human individual obtains complete information, their behavior is determined." The Rationality theorem and the Selfishness theorem (utility maximization under constraints) together form the "rational agent principle." Under this axiom it is the **behavioral reading** of the definition of completeness: complete information = everything required for the judgment has been expressed; determined behavior = determined adjudication. If adjudication remains uncertain even after the information is complete, then the so-called "correctness" is merely an unexposed incompleteness.

**Self-reference theorem.** **Self-reference must be complete; mutable self-reference is forbidden.** This axiom adjudicates itself: its correctness = whether everything required for its judgment has been expressed — decision rules, precedents, layering (scientific layer/textual layer), and the code provisions are all expressed, so self-adjudication closes the case. This axiom has two layers in one: at the scientific layer it is an absolute truth (see below), at the textual layer it is advanced as a scientific commitment — it commits to correctness and bears the burden of delivery.

**Absolute truth theorem (0, 1).** Absolute truth exists — at the very least the Zeroth Axiom and the First Axiom of The Principle are absolute truths; this is not conditioned on human understanding or acceptance.

**Extraterritorial enforcement theorem (0, 1, 2, 3, 4).** The Principle does not pursue non-existence deeply, and therefore does not pursue erroneous things deeply either. Its "therefore" follows by substituting the Zeroth Axiom's definition (non-existence = wrong existence). **Universal adjudication, limited pursuit**: every candidate receives a verdict, and once the verdict is rendered the matter stops — the verdict (not docketed / erroneous existence) already contains every element the judgment requires; pursuing it further yields no new verdict. This is not inaction but exhaustion of the elements. Pruning and disposal belong to the engineering layer (Pruning theorem), and punishment belongs to the scientific secularism law.

**Self-reference and paradox precedents.** By the Self-reference theorem, paradox sentences come to trial as follows:

| Object | Verdict | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Liar sentence ("this sentence is false / does not exist") | Mutable self-reference, self-negating truth value, boundary confusion; it exists as an expressive structure, as a truth-bearer it is not docketed | Pruned (erroneous existence), and not pursued further |
| Russell-style totality ("the set of all propositions") | No boundary ("everything" is not a completed totality; case by case it forms no boundary) | Not docketed |
| Gödel sentence | Not a counterexample but a boundary-revision instruction: it points out the gap in the adjudicating power of the current boundary B | Truth value not adjudicated; referred to boundary revision (Darwinian boundary principle, engineering layer) |

The everyday form of the precedents is exactly the three states of "loophole": the liar sentence is a hole that leaks (mutable self-reference), the Russell-style totality is a room with no walls (no boundary), the Gödel sentence is a hole today's walls cannot patch (an instruction to rebuild). **Patching a loophole = adjusting the boundary** — the folk engineering vocabulary and the Darwinian boundary principle are word for word the same; pruning, patching, and redrawing the boundary are three names for one engineering operation.

**The Gödel position.** On the basis of the First Axiom, The Principle does not accept Gödel's theorem and does not acknowledge its deductive logic or scientific status. At the same time this is not an ostrich act before the greatest challenge: The Principle launches the **Hilbert process**, resolving the Gödel problem technically within a system its opponents accept, by **Gödel-world minimization** (splitting mathematics into a Gödel world G and a Hilbert world H, with G/H → 0; this is a conjecture). Incompleteness is not the boundary of truth but an engineering problem of the current expressive space or the current boundary division: by adjusting boundaries, strengthening expression, and introducing better formalizations, the undecidable domain should be compressed — that is, keep patching loopholes. **Darwinian boundary principle**: adjusting a thing's boundary is an effective means of evolving toward completeness.

**Pruning theorem.** Efficient exploratory engineering must prune: incomplete "correctness," non-ignorant "freedom," non-efficient "justice," "meaning" without a price, and purposeless "evolution." Pruning belongs to **engineering**, not to the Principle's adjudication — the Principle is the courthouse, and order beyond the courthouse still requires dispatching large numbers of police officers, or even armies. Pruning incomplete "correctness" is the theorem form of the everyday engineering phrase "patch the loopholes." Guard against degeneration: pruning may proceed only on whether the topic lies within the boundary, never on whether it is easy — pruning by difficulty is boundary confusion (difficulty drifts with the adjudicator), and freezing pruning violates the Second Axiom.

**Scientific laws.**

- **Scientific truth law**: scientific principles must be absolutely correct.
- **Scientific theorem law**: scientific principles must be committed to correctness.
- **Scientific eternity law**: scientific principles must not change. This does not conflict with "if something better appears, absorb it": this is the relationship between "science" and "the human text of science" — science does not change, text can be revised.
- **Scientific system law**: no scientific principle may oppose itself, nor oppose any other scientific principle.
- **Scientific deduction law**: science must not be challenged by non-scientific deduction. The internal channel for counterexamples is open: you can check counterexamples easily — one is enough.

**Relation to the Second Axiom.** The First Axiom defines correctness (a static criterion); the Second Axiom, "things tend toward completeness," describes the process of approaching correctness (a dynamic trend):

> **The First Axiom defines correctness; the Second Axiom describes the process of approaching correctness.**

**Summary.** The First Axiom accomplishes three things:

1. **It turns "correctness" from a subjective judgment into a structural criterion**: correct = complete = everything required for the judgment has been expressed — in everyday terms: correctness admits no loophole;
2. **It turns "completeness" from an ideal state into a necessary condition of correctness**: a closing condition, not a covering condition;
3. **It supplies a unified constraint for science, alignment, games, and meaning**: any system claiming correctness must answer — have all the elements required to judge it been expressed? If anything remains undecided (if there is a loophole), it is not yet correct.

## Notes

### 1. Completeness is a closing condition, not a covering condition

"Everything required for the judgment has been expressed" ≠ "contains everything." A text need not be omniscient; it only needs to carry its own manual of judgment: criteria, rules, procedures, and precedents all lie within the expression, and the judgment borrows nothing from outside. This is the synonymous restatement of "form = the criterion lies within the system itself": **completeness = formal closure.** It forms a dual with the Zeroth Axiom's "expressible" (object side): the completeness of expression is the shared skeleton of the two axioms — first express the object side in full (admission), then express the criterion side in full (correctness). This is why "correctness is completeness" must come after "existence is difference."

### 2. Case-by-case application: why neither Gödel nor Church constitutes a collision

Completeness as a criterion applies **case by case**; it neither needs nor promises a uniform decision algorithm. Gödel limits "proof" (unprovable sentences within a fixed, effective axiomatization); Church limits "a uniform decision procedure" (no algorithm decides all propositions) — neither limits case-by-case adjudication. Universal jurisdiction in this sense means "every case can be adjudicated," not that "everything" is adjudicated as a completed totality (the latter see row two of the precedent table).

### 3. The naive semantic anchor: correctness admits no loophole

A four-step inference:

1. To say in everyday speech that an argument "has a loophole" means that there is some place at which the judgment falls through — it cannot cover, cannot block, cannot be put clearly;
2. "Correctness admits no loophole": correctness tolerates no place where the judgment falls through;
3. Judgment falling through = what the judgment requires has not been expressed (no verdict / no ground / no test);
4. Hence correct = everything required for the judgment has been expressed = complete.

The crux is step 1: "loophole" is already defined in everyday life by judgment falling through (you can point to a loophole precisely because there the judgment cannot proceed), so no additional bridge is needed between everyday judgment and the definition of completeness — the definition of completeness is already buried in everyday semantics.

**The correspondence between the three states of "loophole" and boundary failure** (reading limitation: "loophole" is taken in the argumentative sense "your argument has a hole," broadly covering the three states, and not in the narrow sense of a security vulnerability):

| Everyday | System term | Form of the judgment falling through |
|---|---|---|
| Has a hole / is missing a piece | No boundary | Not separated out from the background, no place to judge |
| Leaks / cannot be blocked | Boundary leakage | Attribution falls through |
| Vague / cannot be put clearly | Boundary confusion | The verdict falls through |

The everyday vocabulary for defects (loophole, gap, limit, vagueness) is almost entirely boundary vocabulary — language had already written "existence speaks in terms of boundaries" into the lexicon, and "correctness admits no loophole" is this lexicon's direct demand on "correctness."

**Three doorways to the same definition**:

| Reading | Entrance | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Common-sense reading | Correctness admits no loophole | Naive semantic intuition |
| Behavioral reading | With information complete, behavior is determined | Rationality theorem (genealogy) |
| Structural reading | Everything required for the judgment has been expressed | The definition itself |

**Self-applicability**: the work of this document itself — filling in definitions, clearing definitional debt, giving precedents — is literally patching the loopholes of the text. "Correctness admits no loophole" applies to this sentence itself: the day the definitional debt is cleared is the day this document has no loopholes.

### 4. Note on the Rationality theorem

Original: "when a human individual obtains complete information, their behavior is determined." Edge cases (such as the decision when several optimal options tie) belong to the question of decision rules and may be read as "determined up to the optimal set." The Rationality theorem together with the Selfishness theorem is called the "rational agent principle," the cornerstone of modern microeconomics.

### 5. Defense against circular definition

The system is self-referential, and defines over the entire domain: circular definition or circular argument is not in itself impermissible, merely possibly bad — but since the Principle defines over the entire domain, it does not even rise to "bad." The Self-reference theorem requires that self-reference be complete: the elements required to judge itself (decision rules, precedents, layering, code) have all been expressed, and the circularity closes.

### 6. The two layers of absolute truth

The Absolute truth theorem says "not conditioned on human understanding or acceptance" — this is built into the definition of completeness: "everything required for the judgment has been expressed" is a structural state, expression is the presentation of a difference structure, and there is no knower and no accepter in the sentence. The independent content of the theorem is an existence claim: "absolute truth exists, (0, 1) is an instance." The layered structure is found in Note 7 of the Zeroth Axiom: the scientific layer makes the assertion (absolute truth), the textual layer provides the procedure (commitment + delivery + counterexample channel).

### 7. The demarcation of science

**Love-of-wisdom boundary law** (Russell's boundary law): the passage from uncertainty to certainty is the boundary between philosophy and science. **Love-of-wisdom freedom law**: philosophy has no right or wrong. The Principle does not endorse falsificationism: philosophy of science itself no longer upholds falsificationism. The positive criterion of demarcation is the commitment/delivery double axis (science = there is commitment, there is delivery), not falsifiability.

### 8. First value: credit

A product of the commitment/delivery axis. Those who commit to correctness and deliver accumulate credit, and credit is the first value within the system — it is the unit of account by which the structure "there is commitment, there is delivery" can keep running. **Hegel's theorem** (attached to (1)): the only lesson humanity learns from history is that it learns nothing from history — this is the macroscopic manifestation of a broken delivery chain.

### 9. Trustworthy deduction must be based on axioms

Its basis of judgment must be traceable to explicit, stable fundamental principles (otherwise don't bullshit me). This is the traceability clause of "interpretable": the criterion is within the boundary, and the criterion of the criterion is traceable to an axiom.

### 10. Superposition theorem

An engineering method for knowledge extraction (attached to (1)). Paired with the Pruning theorem: superposition extracts, pruning converges.

### 11. Alignment: the Principle rather than humans

"Align with the Principle, not humans" is the ultimate solution to hallucination (**conjecture**). Direction: with human preference as the alignment target, the system inherits human incompleteness (contradiction, drift, non-deliverability); with the Principle as the target, the system is constrained to structures that can be complete, verifiable, and closable. Note that it is an application/conjecture; its behavioral content depends on the unfolding of the Third through Seventh Axioms and is not a direct corollary of the First Axiom.

### 13. Summary

The First Axiom is the criterion clause of "correct metaphysics": **correctness is completeness, completeness is everything required for the judgment has been expressed — in everyday terms: correctness admits no loophole.** It holds of itself (Self-reference theorem), it holds of the assertion of absolute truth (Absolute truth theorem), it adjudicates without pursuing (Extraterritorial enforcement theorem), it gives precedents for paradoxes, and it gives both a position and a process for Gödel. The pruning and disposal that follow adjudication are left to the police and armies beyond the courthouse.
