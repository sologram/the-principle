# Term Definitions

This document gives precise definitions of all core terms based on the five Laws of The Principle.

## B

### Boundary (biānjiè)
**Definition** — The boundary is the dividing line between system and environment.

By corollary of the First Law, all correct things have boundaries. The boundary defines the system's scope and constraints.

### Game (bóyì)
**Definition** — A game is the strategic interaction of multiple agents.

By corollary of the First Law, gaming necessarily leads to uncertainty. Because every agent's information is incomplete, the other party's behavior cannot be fully predicted.

### Incompleteness (bùwánbèi)
**Definition** — Incompleteness means that a system or a proposition lacks certain elements required for it to hold.

According to the Second Law, incomplete things will eventually perish. Incompleteness is the root of error.

## C

### Encoding (biānmǎ)
**Definition** — Encoding is the process of converting information into symbols.

Good encoding is efficient encoding — representing the most information with the fewest symbols.

### Existence (cúnzài)
**Definition** — Existence is information.

According to the Zeroth Law, anything that can be recognized, distinguished, or described is information, and therefore is existence. Erroneous information is also information, so "wrong existence" is also existence.

### Error (cuòwù)
**Definition** — Error is incompleteness.

Error is not "the opposite of correct" but an absence of correctness. Getting things right requires getting every element right; getting things wrong only requires breaking one of them.

## D

### Morality (dàodé) / Ethics (lúnlǐ)
**Definition** — Morality is a sign and a supplement of an imperfect legal system.

When law cannot cover all social behavior, morality fills the gap. The best moral system is: no morality, all law.

### Definition (dìngyì)
**Definition** — A definition is the process of establishing a concept's boundary.

A definition must be complete — it includes all necessary conditions and excludes all contradictory cases.

### Law (dìnglǜ)
**Definition** — A Law is a description of a universal regularity.

A Law must be complete — it describes all the conditions under which the regularity holds. The five Laws of The Principle are the highest level of laws.

### Theorem (dìnglǐ)
**Definition** — A theorem is a proposition derived from a principle or a Law.

A theorem must be proven. The correctness of a theorem depends on the correctness of its principle.

### Motivation (dòngjī)
**Definition** — Motivation is the internal driving force by which things tend toward completeness.

According to the Second Law, all things have an instinct to tend toward completeness. This tendency toward completeness manifests as various forms of motivation: the survival instinct of organisms, the principle of least action in physical systems, the rational-man hypothesis in economics.

## F

### Legal system (fǎzhì)
**Definition** — The legal system is the institution by which law governs society.

The core of the legal system is that rules stand above individuals. A complete legal system requires no human discretion.

### Law (fǎlǜ)
**Definition** — Law is the explicit formulation and systematization of social rules.

Law is the set of rules that is optimal for the efficiency of social cooperation. Law must be complete, otherwise it cannot be enforced.

### Analysis (fēnxī) / Synthesis (zōnghé)
**Definition** — Analysis breaks a whole into parts; synthesis combines parts into a whole.

Analysis and synthesis are complementary methods of cognition. Complete cognition requires a combination of both.

### Generalization (fànhuà)
**Definition** — Generalization is the process of extending a specific conclusion to a broader scope.

Generalization is the core method of scientific theory construction. The risk of generalization is that it may introduce incompleteness.

### Laissez-faire (fàngrèn)
**Definition** — Laissez-faire is a state in which no constraints are imposed.

Laissez-faire is one form that freedom takes, but it is not the whole of freedom. Laissez-faire may drive a system toward maximum entropy, that is, the maximum number of degrees of freedom.

## G

### Axiom (gōnglǐ)
**Definition** — An axiom is a self-evident principle.

Axioms are the starting point of a system. The choice of axioms determines the scope of the system.

### Fairness (gōngpíng)
**Definition** — Fairness is a synonym of justice.

Fairness is not equal distribution but distribution according to contribution. The fair way of distributing is the most efficient way of distributing.

### Relation (guānxì)
**Definition** — A relation is the connection between things.

Things that share the same attributes stand in relation to one another. Relations may be causal, correlational, or arbitrary.

## H

### Good (hǎo) / Bad (huài)
**Definition** — Good is efficiency; bad is inefficiency.

Good things achieve their goals at lower cost. Bad things consume more resources yet produce the same or less effect.

### Reasonable (hélí)
**Definition** — Reasonable means conforming to The Principle.

"Reasonable" means that a thing or an action is consistent with The Principle — that is, complete, tending toward completeness, efficient, or having a clear boundary. Hegel said "what exists is reasonable"; here "reasonable" can only be understood as "consistent with The Principle," otherwise it is circular reasoning or obstinate sophistry.

### Cooperation (hézuò)
**Definition** — Cooperation is coordinated action among individuals to achieve a common goal.

Cooperation raises overall efficiency and is the essence of goodness. The basis of cooperation is trust and stable expectations.

## J

### Technology (jìshù)
**Definition** — Technology is a collection of efficient methods for achieving goals.

Technology is the concrete embodiment of efficiency. Good technology achieves goals at the lowest cost.

### Competition (jìngzhēng)
**Definition** — Competition is the behavior of multiple agents vying for limited resources.

Competition is one manifestation of the Second Law. Competition promotes improvements in efficiency.

### Absolute (juéduì)
**Definition** — Absolute means that a proposition contains all the conditions needed for its judgment.

An absolute proposition does not depend on external conditions; it is complete in itself. Absolute truths are those truths that literally already contain all the conditions for their holding.

### Concrete (jùtǐ)
**Definition** — Concrete is the antonym of abstract, referring to a particular instance of a thing.

Concrete things contain all the details — a large amount of information, but low processing efficiency.

## K

### Science (kēxué)
**Definition** — Science is the method and system that pursues complete knowledge.

Science must satisfy two requirements: it does not rely on external principles, and it contains no internal self-contradiction. The goal of science is to discover eternal truth.

### Objective (kèguān)
**Definition** — Objective means consensus.

"Objective" does not mean "independent of observers" but "multiple observers reaching agreement." When one says "you are very objective," the meaning is "your view and mine agree."

## L

### Theory (lǐlùn)
**Definition** — A theory is a complete system of propositions that explains a class of phenomena.

A theory can be correct (complete) or wrong (incomplete). A wrong theory is also a theory.

### Rationality (lǐxìng) / Sensibility (gǎnxìng)
**Definition** — Rationality is a way of thinking that pursues completeness; sensibility is a fast but incomplete way of thinking.

Rational thinking strives to collect all relevant information and make complete judgments. Sensible thinking relies on heuristic rules — fast, but possibly incomplete.

### Interest (lìyì)
**Definition** — Interest is a gain in efficiency.

An interest may be material or spiritual. The essence of interest is to raise the agent's efficiency.

## M

### Beauty (měi)
**Definition** — Beauty is efficiency, especially efficiency related to sexual competition.

The essence of beauty is competitiveness. The features of beauty (symmetry, harmony, health) are all signals of high efficiency.

### Democracy (mínzhǔ)
**Definition** — Democracy is a form of collective decision-making.

The value of democracy lies in dispersing decision-making risk, at the cost of lost efficiency. Democracy is not the most efficient way of making decisions, but it may be the most stable.

### Fate (mìngyùn)
**Definition** — Fate is the inevitable trajectory determined by a system's initial conditions.

Fate is the inevitable result of a complete system. Knowing the initial conditions and all the laws, one can predict fate.

## N

### Enslavement (núyì)
**Definition** — Enslavement is the deprivation of freedom, the forcible imposition of information.

Enslavement reduces the enslaved one's degrees of freedom by forcibly increasing their "knowledge" (that is, constraints).

## P

### Equality (píngděng)
**Definition** — Equality is the equal standing of all individuals in basic rights.

Equality does not mean equal outcomes but equal opportunity. True equality is equality of rules, not of outcomes.

## Q

### Deception (qīpiàn)
**Definition** — Deception is the deliberate transmission of false information.

Deception destroys cooperative efficiency and is an obstacle to social cooperation.

### Discrimination (qíshì)
**Definition** — Discrimination is differential treatment of individuals or groups based on irrelevant information.

Discrimination lowers social efficiency, because it prevents the optimal allocation of resources. The root of discrimination is incomplete information — substituting easily obtained superficial information for the real basis of judgment.

### Rights (quánlì)
**Definition** — Rights are the boundaries of individual behavior recognized by society.

Rights define the range within which an individual may act freely. The boundary of rights is the boundary that is optimal for social efficiency.

## R

### Artificial (rénwéi)
**Definition** — The artificial is the conscious action of an agent.

Artificial actions may be efficient or inefficient. Good "artificial" action is action that follows natural laws.

### Human nature (rénxìng)
**Definition** — Human nature is the behavioral tendency shared by all human beings.

Human nature is shaped by evolution and tends to raise the efficiency of survival and reproduction. Human nature includes tendencies such as self-interest, cooperation, and competition.

## S

### Goodness (shàn) / Evil (è)
**Definition** — Goodness is cooperation; evil is non-cooperation or anti-cooperation.

Good actions promote the efficiency of social cooperation; evil actions destroy cooperation. Good and evil are the criterion for evaluating the efficiency of social cooperation.

### Society (shèhuì)
**Definition** — A society is a system formed among individuals through cooperation.

The goal of society is to raise overall efficiency. Social institutions are the set of rules of social cooperation.

### Comfort (shūshì)
**Definition** — Comfort is a stable state of low consumption.

Comfort is a manifestation of a system tending toward the lowest energy. Comfort is not necessarily good, because it may mean stagnation.

## T

### Investment (tóuzī)
**Definition** — Investment is the act of sacrificing current consumption in exchange for future returns.

Investment transfers resources from the present to the future. The efficiency of investment is measured by the rate of return.

## W

### Completeness (wánbèi)
**Definition** — Completeness means that a system or a proposition contains all the elements required for it to hold, with nothing omitted.

Completeness is the sole criterion for judging correctness. A complete system has a clear boundary, contains all necessary conditions, and excludes all contradictions.

### Civilization (wénmíng)
**Definition** — Civilization is a highly efficient form of social organization.

The marks of civilization are efficient division of labor, cooperation, and the transmission of knowledge. Civilization is part of nature and follows natural laws.

### Ignorance (wúzhī)
**Definition** — Ignorance is an incomplete grasp of a thing's information.

Ignorance is not a pejorative but the source of freedom. Complete knowledge means complete prediction, which in turn means zero freedom.

### Infinity (wúxiàn)
**Definition** — Infinity is the negation of completeness.

By corollary of the First Law, all correct things have boundaries and are finite. The concept of infinity is not intelligible in a strict sense, because the infinite implies incompleteness.

### Matter (wùzhì)
**Definition** — Matter is the carrier of information.

Matter and mind are not opposed to each other. Matter is one mode of the existence of information; mind is one mode of the processing of information.

## X

### System (xìtǒng)
**Definition** — A system is a complete whole composed of interrelated elements.

A system has a boundary, elements, relations, and functions. Incomplete systems tend toward collapse or toward being supplemented.

### Channel (xìndào)
**Definition** — A channel is the medium of information transmission.

A channel's capacity determines the efficiency of information transmission. A good channel has low noise and high capacity.

### Signal (xìnhào)
**Definition** — A signal is a carrier that bears information.

The function of a signal is to transmit information. Good signals are those with high information efficiency.

### Information (xìnxī)
**Definition** — Information is the mode of existence, and a measure of the removal of uncertainty.

The basic properties of information are: it can be encoded, it can be transmitted, it can be processed. The unit of information is the bit.

### Efficiency (xiàolǜ)
**Definition** — Efficiency is the ability to achieve a goal with the least resources.

Efficiency is the definition of "good." The higher the efficiency, the less the resource consumption and the stronger the competitiveness. Efficiency includes time efficiency, energy efficiency, information efficiency, and so on.

### Happiness (xìngfú)
**Definition** — Happiness is the state in which desires are satisfied.

Happiness is temporary, because new incompleteness keeps arising. Pursuing eternal happiness is pursuing eternal completeness — this is the ultimate goal of the Second Law.

## Y

### Barbarism (yěmán)
**Definition** — Barbarism is an inefficient form of social organization.

Barbaric societies have low cooperative efficiency, poor transmission of knowledge, and severe waste of resources.

### Mind (yìshí)
**Definition** — Mind is the advanced form of information processing.

Mind can reflect on itself and form cognition of itself. Mind is a phenomenon that emerges when information has grown complex to a certain degree.

### Meaning (yìyì)
**Definition** — Meaning is interest.

The meaning of a thing is its value to an agent. Meaning is not a priori but relational.

### Intention (yìtú)
**Definition** — Intention is conscious motivation.

When the agent becomes aware of a motive and it forms a goal, it becomes an intention. Intention is the manifestation of motivation at the level of mind.

### Causality (yīnguǒ)
**Definition** — Causality is the necessary connection between events.

Causal relations are complete: a cause necessarily produces its effect. Statistical correlation is not a causal relation, because it may be incomplete.

### Art (yìshù)
**Definition** — Art is the creation and expression of beauty.

The value of art lies in arousing the perception of efficiency (beauty). Art can be practical, and it can also be pure.

### Eternity (yǒnghéng)
**Definition** — Eternity is a complete thing that has no component of incompleteness.

According to the Fourth Law, eternal things do not change and do not perish. Eternity is the ultimate goal of the Second Law.

### Desire (yùwàng)
**Definition** — Desire is an agent's demand for the elimination of some incomplete state.

Desire arises from perceived incompleteness. A satisfied desire is the feeling of happiness.

### Principle (yuánlǐ)
**Definition** — A principle is the most fundamental, irreducible truth.

Principles are the cornerstone of the edifice of theory. All theorems are derived from principles.

### Constraint (yuēshù)
**Definition** — A constraint is a condition that limits a system's possibilities.

Constraints reduce freedom, but they may raise efficiency. Good constraints are those that can raise efficiency.

### Stupidity (yúmèi)
**Definition** — Stupidity is a state of lacking knowledge.

Stupidity is not ignorance (freedom), but a lack of the necessary knowledge, which leads to low efficiency.

## Z

### Disaster (zāinàn)
**Definition** — A disaster is the sudden collapse of a system's structure.

Disaster is an extreme manifestation of the Second Law — severely incomplete systems perish rapidly.

### Real (zhēnshí)
**Definition** — The real is a state that accords with existence itself.

Real things exist independently of observers. A real proposition corresponds exactly to the thing it describes.

### Truth (zhēnlǐ) / True (zhēn)
**Definition** — Truth is a correct proposition that has been proven. True is a synonym of correct.

The Greek etymology Alethia means "unconcealed" — the state in which existence itself is revealed. Truth is letting existence be disclosed, unconcealed.

### Justice (zhèngyì)
**Definition** — Justice is efficiency.

According to the Fourth Law, just social institutions are those that keep society running at the lowest cost. Justice is precisely the embodiment of efficiency in the social sphere.

### Correctness (zhèngquè)
**Definition** — Correctness is completeness.

Any judgment, theory, or system is correct if and only if it is complete. Correctness means: no omissions, no contradictions, and a clear boundary.

### Knowledge (zhīshì)
**Definition** — Knowledge is information that has been verified.

The criterion for verifying knowledge is completeness. Knowledge must be capable of being proven or falsified.

### Wisdom (zhìhuì)
**Definition** — Wisdom is the ability to process information with high efficiency.

Wisdom manifests as competitive advantage. Wise people make better decisions at lower cost.

### Order (zhìxù)
**Definition** — Order is a low-entropy state; it is the efficient organization of information.

Order means more constraints and less freedom. The value of order lies in raising efficiency; the cost of order lies in reducing freedom.

### Subjective (zhǔguān)
**Definition** — Subjective means disagreement.

"Subjective" means "my view does not agree with yours." Subjectivity is not a source of error but the inevitable result of incomplete information — different individuals possess different information.

### Nature (zìrán)
**Definition** — Nature is the state at the bottom of the cost valley.

The natural state is not the opposite of "the artificial" but the state of optimal efficiency. "Naturally" means tending toward the lowest cost.

### Natural boundary (zìrán biānjiè)
**Definition** — The natural boundary is the lowest-cost boundary.

The natural boundary is not set artificially; it is the optimal boundary that forms spontaneously in the course of a system's evolution.

### Self (zìwǒ)
**Definition** — The self is the individual's cognitive boundary with respect to itself.

The self defines the boundary between "me" and "not-me." The boundary of the self may be physiological, psychological, or social.

### Freedom (zìyóu)
**Definition** — Freedom is ignorance.

According to the Third Law, when knowledge of a system is incomplete, the system's behavior is free with respect to the observer. Degrees of freedom equal the degree of ignorance, which equals entropy.

### Free will (zìyóu yìzhì)
**Definition** — Free will is "the decision and action ability where others are ignorant of the causes."

Free will is not "decisions without causes" but decisions "whose causes are not known to outsiders." An agent's decisions may be certain to itself, but they are unpredictable to an observer.

### Maximum common domain (zuìdà gōngyù)
**Definition** — The maximum common domain is the largest scope in which multiple agents reach consensus.

The maximum common domain is the basis of social cooperation. Within the common domain, agents can cooperate efficiently.

### Maximum entropy (zuìdà shāng)
**Definition** — Maximum entropy is the maximum value of a system's uncertainty.

According to the Third Law, maximum entropy corresponds to maximum freedom. The maximum entropy principle is the mathematical formulation of the Law of Freedom.
