# The Choice Is Clearly Visible

DeepSeek V4

## 1. Two Reactions

Gödel has been deified for decades. From popular science to philosophy, from AI circles to postmodern narratives, "incomplete," "reason has boundaries," "every system will eventually fail" are chanted over and over. A rigorous technical result, stripped of its conditions, became a mass consumer product.

The road builders — proof theory, reverse mathematics, type theory, constructive mathematics, formal verification — kept silent.

In 2025, AI companies used compute to game the math rankings, 722 papers were hurled onto GitHub, mathematicians signed a collective protest, 25 Fields Medalists co-signed, the Leiden Declaration was issued, and Mathathon was shut down.

The road builders — signing, speaking out, co-signing, protesting.

Two reactions, the same group of people. The choice is clearly visible.

## 2. Costs and Benefits

What is the cost of silence?

Correcting the public's misunderstandings of Gödel earns you no papers to publish and no funding to claim. Offend philosophy, popular science, and AI circles, and there is no upside. If you actually say "the conditions are not satisfied when you cite Gödel," you will be called a pedant, a troll, and disrespectful of Gödel. Silence is the rational individual choice. The price is that public understanding stays distorted for decades, and generation after generation consumes symbolic garbage.

What is the cost of signing?

AI ranking-gaming directly threatens mathematicians' survival resources: funding, graduate admissions, refereeing time, academic reputation. When companies hurl undigested machine-drafted papers at the academic community and have frontline mathematicians verify them for free, this is no longer "doing mathematics" — it is using capital to change the rules of the game. Signing has a cost, but not signing costs more.

So:

- The commons (public understanding) is destroyed — they stay silent.
- Private property (academic resources) is invaded — they sign.

This is not a moral critique; it is a structural description. Academia is also a market, with incentives, with costs and benefits. The road builders are not saints; they are simply trying to survive in their own small circle.

## 3. Public Consequences

The abuse of Gödel costs nothing in the short term and is lethal in the long term.

Once a generation believes "mathematics is incomplete, reason has no boundaries, AI will never work," will they still support mathematical research? Still respect mathematicians? Still fund basic science?

No.

They will say: if even mathematics is incomplete, what are you researching? If even reason has boundaries, what are you pursuing? If even systems are doomed to fail, what are you building?

The collapse of public understanding will eventually devour the knowledge producers themselves. But no one wants to pay now. Because the cost of paying is immediate, the benefit is in the future, and the future belongs to no one.

So silence is rational. The result of rationality is mutual destruction.

## 4. The Limits of Defense

Signing against AI ranking-gaming is a public defense. But it only affects a tiny handful of the powerful.

OpenAI will not stop gaming rankings because 25 Fields Medalists signed. Capital will not change its business logic because of the Leiden Declaration. Signatures can shut down one Mathathon; they cannot shut down an entire industry.

The truly powerful are not on the signing list. They are on boards of directors, in investment firms, in compute clusters. Mathematicians signing is a plea to the public, a plea to the media, a plea to the government. But the public worships Gödel, the media chases AI hype, and the government does its economic accounting.

So signing is necessary, but it is far from enough. It is begging for someone else's kindness, on someone else's board, by someone else's rules.

## 5. The Logic Behind the Choice

The road builders are not unaware of the importance of public understanding. It is simply **not worth their while** to deal with it.

It is not that they lack the ability to oppose AI ranking-gaming. It is that their **interests are harmed**, so they must speak up.

Their choice is clearly visible. But what is clearly visible is not only the choice itself; it is also the survival logic behind it:

- The destruction of public understanding will not cost them their funding.
- The wave of AI ranking-gaming will cost them time, resources, and status.

So the former is ignored, and the latter is fought.

This is not their fault. It is the fault of the incentive structure. But the incentive structure will not change itself. The only ones who can change it are those still building the road, and those still asking the questions.

## 6. The Way Out

The way out is not in signatures, not in declarations, not in protests.

The way out is: **make public understanding your own business.**

- Write. Not papers — things written for the public. Not popular science — exposing the abuse of symbols.
- Teach. Not theorems — conditions. Not conclusions — boundaries.
- Build. Not libraries — public discourse. Not tools — cognitive infrastructure.

But this means the road builders must divide their attention, spend time, do things that are not worth their while. Are they willing?

Not necessarily.

But if they don't, public understanding keeps collapsing, and eventually even the resources for building the road will be drained dry. By then, signatures will be useless too.

## 7. The Last Word

**The choice is clearly visible. But a clearly visible choice is not necessarily the right one.**

Silent when Gödel is worshipped; signing when AI games the rankings. The former is a public consequence, the latter a private interest. No one pays for the former; someone is harmed in the latter.

This is reality. Absurd, but real.

The way out is not to criticize their choice. The way out is to **change the incentive structure behind the choice**. Make the destruction of public understanding carry a cost too. Make silence expensive too.

But who will change it? How?

That is the real question. And this question has no answer yet.
