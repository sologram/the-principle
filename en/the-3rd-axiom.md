# The Third Axiom of Games

## Text

**Third Axiom: Games are turbulence.**

> **Game = strategic interaction among multiple agents; turbulence = the continuous churn of failed coordination. Games are turbulence: whenever there is strategic interaction among multiple agents, there is turbulence.**

**Everyday anchor: too many cooks spoil the broth.** Many mouths mean many differences, and difficulty agreeing means coordination failure — turbulence is the normal condition, not a pathological one. The specific form of coordination failure is "**three monks have no water**": each waits for the others and the actions cancel one another. The spillover of turbulence is "**when gods fight, mortals suffer**" — bystanders are caught in it and social waste is produced (the transaction costs of the Fifth Axiom).

**Position of the axiom.** The Third Axiom is the **multi-agent dynamics** of meta-science, the fourth link of the four-link chain:

> Zeroth Axiom (**existence entrance**: subject terms, boundaries) → First Axiom (**correctness adjudication**: completeness) → Second Axiom (**direction of approach**: dynamics) → Third Axiom (**multi-agent dynamics**: game turbulence).

The first three handle the existence, correctness, and evolutionary direction of a single system; this one introduces multi-agent interaction and reveals the instability endogenous to it. It is at the same time the middle segment of the mechanism chain (following the Second Axiom):

> **Incomplete → information blockage → turbulence → selection → tending toward completeness.**

This axiom takes up "blockage → turbulence" and hands "turbulence → selection" to the Second Axiom: turbulence is the source of selection pressure; without turbulence, selection stalls.

**Core meaning.** "Game" is not the everyday "playing games" but **any strategic interaction among multiple decision-makers whose interests are not fully aligned** — economics (bidding, competition, trading), biology (predation, parasitism, competition for resources), society (elections, negotiation, disputes, academic competition), and computation (algorithmic conflict, attack and defense, adversarial training) are all on the list. Turbulence is a **structural characteristic** of games, with three components:

| Component | Effect |
|---|---|
| Mutual strategic dependence | Coordination is valuable: each agent's optimal strategy depends on the others' strategies |
| Incomplete alignment of interests | Coordination is unnatural: cooperation is not the natural equilibrium |
| Information blockage | Coordination is impossible: the others' current choices are invisible, and agents can only decide on local observations |

Superimposed, the three make the state oscillate continuously, switch, and reorganize — that is, turbulence. **Degree of turbulence = degree of information blockage**: with blockage at zero, turbulence converges; with blockage irreducible, turbulence is irreducible. Note that the irreducibility belongs to **blockage** (the invisibility of others' current choices, the limits of capability, the cost of unblocking), not to the multi-agent structure itself — the structure makes coordination valuable, blockage makes coordination impossible.

**The Games theorem: turbulence originates from information blockage.** The axiom states the phenomenon (games produce turbulence); the theorem gives the mechanism (blockage is the cause). Information blockage = what the judgment requires has not been expressed (First Axiom): the agent cannot obtain the information needed for coordination, each makes a "best response" on incomplete information, and the superposition becomes system-level unpredictability. Hence:

> **Information blockage makes an agent's "best response" rest on erroneous existence, and this superposes into system-level turbulence.**

Short-run predictability holds (modeling within a limited window works), long-run unpredictability holds (reflexivity and blockage guarantee that prediction fails), and statistical regularities are identifiable (individuals are unpredictable, but the statistical characteristics of the system level are stable).

**Source of generalization: the minority game.** N participants each choose one of two options per round, and the minority wins. Three points:

1. **Anti-majority recursion**: the optimal strategy depends on the others' strategies, and they in turn are reasoning about your reasoning — the recursion does not converge;
2. **Reflexivity**: the act of predicting changes the system dynamics, so the prediction fails ("adaptation leads to maladaptation");
3. **No persistent equilibrium**: any strategy, once it becomes mainstream, turns into the majority and thereby fails.

The precise content of blockage becomes visible here: **the others' present choices are invisible**. Reflexivity arises from this — the anti-majority recursion is a chain of reasoning under blockage, and the absence of a persistent equilibrium is its dynamic consequence.

**Table of precedents.**

| Object | Verdict | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Prisoner's dilemma | Information is blocked by the interrogator; the betrayal equilibrium is a product of blockage, not a failure of rationality | The cause is ruled to be blockage; unblocking belongs to the engineering layer (with a postal route, cooperation is feasible) |
| "Game among identical agents" | No difference → degeneracy to a single agent, no game, no turbulence | Not docketed (difference is a precondition of games) |
| The "stable solution" of the minority game | Reflexivity makes any mainstream strategy fail; what is blocked is the others' current choices | Ruled to have no fixed point; no persistent equilibrium |

**The closed loop with the First and Second Axioms.** The First Axiom supplies the standard of correctness: information blockage = what the judgment requires has not been expressed = incomplete, so the "best response" under blockage is in fact an erroneous response. The Second Axiom supplies the direction: turbulence eliminates incomplete strategies and is the selection mechanism of tending toward completeness — turbulence is the crucible in which completeness is increased ("creative destruction" as the analogical term). Combined:

> **The First Axiom defines "correct"; the Third Axiom describes the system-level consequences of agents acting by a (possibly erroneous) standard of correctness under conditions of blockage — and this consequence (turbulence) in turn becomes the selection pressure of the Second Axiom.**

**Pruning theorem: pseudo-freedom and permissiveness.** What pruning cuts is "freedom whose form is not ignorance" — that is, **permissiveness** (wrong ignorance, which is in fact knowledge): knowing and pretending not to know, holding information and withholding it. Note that it is not freedom itself that is cut: freedom = correct ignorance (the quadruple), it is the space of variation; permissiveness is the object of pruning. **The interrogator is the manufacturer of permissiveness**: being in fact knowledge while forcibly manufacturing the erroneous ignorance of the prisoners — the personalized form of blockage. Hence "eliminating information blockage" = pruning permissiveness: release the withheld expression, so that what the judgment requires becomes available.

**Self-reference and scale-freeness.** This axiom refers to itself and applies to itself:

> **Scientific layer**: games are turbulence (the content does not change, by the scientific eternity law). **Textual layer**: The Principle itself sits inside a game — the contest with philosophy and with academia is a multi-agent game, and the acceptance history of the Principle obeys game dynamics.

But the criterion does not follow power: the love-of-wisdom autocracy law (the validity of dialectics derives from the dialectician's power) is the **currency rule** of this game, not a criterion of the Principle — the Principle rests on commitment/delivery (there is commitment, there is delivery), not on power; anti-academia is precisely anti-corruption, a refusal to accept the currency of power. Mutable self-reference is forbidden: "correct" must not be redefined by game/power (the criterion belongs to the First Axiom). **The axiom is scale-free**: turbulence contains no scale parameter — it applies to individuals, organizations, nations, species, algorithms, and markets alike; the expressive space is neutral with respect to matter (weak expression) and mind (strong expression).

**Summary.** The Third Axiom does three things:

1. **It reveals the endogenous instability of games**: strategic interaction among multiple agents naturally produces turbulence; this is a structural property, not a pathology;
2. **It traces the root of turbulence**: turbulence originates from information blockage (Games theorem); degree of turbulence = degree of information blockage;
3. **It connects the single-agent and multi-agent worlds**: the first three axioms describe single systems; this axiom enters the multi-agent world of competition, cooperation, institutions, and markets, supplying a principled basis for institutional design and safety constraints.

## Notes

### 1. Critique of the Nash equilibrium

The Nash equilibrium describes "fixed points"; this axiom describes "the motion itself." Three criticisms:

1. **Multiple equilibria**: one game has several equilibria, and the system's switching among them is itself turbulence;
2. **Inefficient equilibria**: the betrayal equilibrium of the prisoner's dilemma is Pareto-dominated by the cooperative equilibrium — equilibrium does not guarantee correctness (completeness);
3. **Unreachable equilibria**: when blockage prevents coordination, the system may never converge to an equilibrium.

This axiom does not deny that equilibria exist (when the conditions are met), but stresses: static analysis is not dynamic reality; an equilibrium is a slice of the game's fixed point, not the game's normal state.

### 2. Detailed note on the genealogy of the minority game

The minority game is a special case in game theory, but its three points are universal: anti-majority recursion (an infinite chain of reasoning), reflexivity (Soros's sense of reflexivity: the prediction changes the object predicted), and the absence of a persistent equilibrium (mainstream implies failure). Real-world correspondents: crowded trades in financial markets, the flipping of fashion trends. Its profound implication is that **the perpetual engine of turbulence lies not in the irrationality of participants but in the coupling of reflexivity and blockage** — even with everyone rational, the system does not come to rest.

### 3. Formalization: demoted to an engineering-layer annotation source

Formalizations such as strategy spaces, state trajectories, and Lyapunov exponents (chaos, sensitive dependence on initial conditions) are borrowed notation whose criteria do not trace back to axioms (trustworthy deduction must be based on axioms), and the assertion that "long-run behavior contains no fixed point / periodic orbit" is too strong (repeated games can converge on cooperation; rock-paper-scissors is a periodic orbit). Hence formalization is demoted to an **engineering-layer annotation source** (outline precedent: "game solver = efficiency annotation source"): it is used to measure degree of blockage, volatility, and strategy frequencies, and does not enter the definition layer. The three statements in the main text (short-run predictability, long-run unpredictability, identifiability of statistical regularities) are already sufficient; no measure is needed.

### 4. Freedom and permissiveness: the archived reading of pruning

The outline's quadruple: **freedom = correct ignorance; order = correct knowledge; servitude = wrong knowledge; permissiveness = wrong ignorance (in fact knowledge).** The standard reading of the Pruning theorem's "prune freedom that is not ignorance" is thereby nailed down: what is cut is **freedom whose form is not ignorance**, i.e. permissiveness (pseudo-freedom) — mistaking permissiveness for freedom makes pruning read as a crusade against freedom; mistaking "based on correct information" for freedom makes pruning read as a rewriting of freedom into order. Correct reading: freedom (correct ignorance) is retained, permissiveness (wrong ignorance) is pruned.

### 5. Credit and repeated games: the mechanism for unblocking

Prisoners betray in a single-shot game and cooperate in a repeated game — the difference is **credit** (the first value): a record of commitments and deliveries turns "the others' current choices are invisible" into something expected. Credit is a mechanism for unblocking, and price is a mechanism for unblocking (Sixth Axiom: price = information compression); both release withheld expression as a signal that can be judged. The Third Axiom thereby connects to the first value: **the way out of games is not the abolition of games, but the building of infrastructure for unblocking.**

### 6. Reframing the efficient market hypothesis

The efficient market hypothesis = the idealization of "blockage → 0." This axiom's position: the structural turbulence of markets is not a pathology but a measure of blockage — price deviations reflect information blockage (the pricing details belong to the Sixth Axiom). Statistical characteristics such as the volatility distribution and the strategy frequency distribution are identifiable (the third statement in the main text), and these are precisely the objects of quantitative measurement. There is only one principled judgment here: **if blockage cannot be eliminated to zero, a fully efficient market is unreachable, and turbulence is the normal condition.**

### 7. The intersection with evolutionary game theory

Evolutionary game theory describes the evolution of strategy frequencies with replicator dynamics. The intersection: both admit that games produce dynamic change. The divergence: this axiom additionally supplies direction — turbulence selects for more complete strategies (attached to the Second Axiom), while replicator dynamics describes only changes in frequency and contains no completeness criterion. Evolutionary game theory can therefore serve as an engineering-layer tool, while the direction continues to be supplied by the Second Axiom.

### 9. Applications and the conjecture layer

- **A basis for institutional design** (application): good institutions mitigate information blockage and lower the destructive cost of turbulence — the direction is pruning permissiveness, building credit, opening postal routes;
- **AGI safety constraints** (application/conjecture): games among multiple AGI systems may produce uncontrollable turbulence, and this must be taken into account at the design stage;
- **The interrogator model of the prisoner's dilemma** (an engineering extension of the precedent): the minimal action for unblocking is the restoration of communication — "with a postal route, cooperation is feasible."

### 10. Summary

The Third Axiom is the multi-agent dynamics engine of the system: **games are turbulence** — dependence makes coordination valuable, misalignment of interests makes coordination unnatural, blockage makes coordination impossible. Degree of turbulence = degree of information blockage; the precedents give three cases (prisoners, identical agents, minority game); pruning gives the way out (prune permissiveness, build credit, unblock); the axiom itself is in a game, but the criterion does not follow power. It expands the world of the first three axioms into conflict and cooperation between people, between nations, between species, and between algorithms.
