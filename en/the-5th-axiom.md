# The Fifth Axiom of Justice

## Text

**Fifth Axiom: Justice is efficiency.**

> **Justice = efficiency = lowering interaction friction. A society's degree of justice is inversely proportional to its transaction costs (justice measurement theorem).**

**Everyday anchor: even brothers keep clear accounts.** Clear accounts = clear rules = low cost — naive intuition welded "justice" to "accountable arithmetic" long ago. Supporting lines: **an eye for an eye leaves everyone blind** (conflict costs unbounded), **even a good judge can't settle family disputes** (boundary confusion → high cost), **harmony breeds wealth** (goodness = cooperation → friction reduction). All four lines are boundary words and cost words, of the same type as "correctness admits no loophole": the naive semantic-intuitive face of justice is "clear books."

**Position of the axiom.** The Fifth Axiom is the **social criterion** of meta-science, the sixth link of the six-link chain:

> Zeroth Axiom (**entry of existence**) → First Axiom (**judgment of correctness**) → Second Axiom (**direction of approach**) → Third Axiom (**multi-agent dynamics**) → Fourth Axiom (**openness criterion**) → Fifth Axiom (**social criterion**).

It is also the back half of the mechanism chain (following the Third Axiom):

> **Incompleteness → information blockage → turbulence → selection → approach completeness; blockage → information cost → turbulence (= the manifestation of high transaction costs) → institutions (infrastructure for unblocking and friction reduction) → degree of justice = capacity to lower costs.**

The Third Axiom explains "why institutions are needed" (games produce turbulence); the Fifth Axiom explains "what makes an institution good" (an institution that lowers friction is just).

**Core meaning.** Three strokes:

- **Justice is not a moral intuition but a system-level efficiency property**: the "degree of justice" of an institution is determined by its capacity to lower transaction costs and raise system efficiency. Moral intuition supplies no criterion (love-of-wisdom freedom law: philosophy has no right or wrong); the criterion lies on the certain side (love-of-wisdom boundary law: from uncertainty to certainty runs the boundary between philosophy and science).
- **Efficiency is not economic growth**: not GDP or productivity indices, but **institutional efficiency** — the capacity of institutional arrangements to lower the friction of human interaction (transaction costs: information search, negotiation, monitoring, enforcement, conflict resolution).
- **Not utilitarianism**: "greatest happiness" is an indeterminate term (no commitment, no delivery); this axiom relies on no happiness assumption — justice = friction reduction, a testable engineering proposition.

**Good = efficiency.** The outline's four criteria in the definitions section land here:

> **Truth = correctness (First Axiom); good = efficiency (this axiom); goodness = cooperation (Third/Fifth Axioms); beauty = competitiveness (Second/Seventh Axioms).**

This completes the takeover of the normative problem-domain: the intra-system ground for "justice needs no moral foundation" is not philosophical critique but **division of labor** — truth and good each have their criterion (completeness / efficiency), goodness is cooperation, beauty is competitiveness, each under its own axiom. "Justice is efficiency" is the correct metaphysical move by which meta-science takes over the normative problem-domain.

**Justice measurement theorem.**

> **A society's degree of justice is inversely proportional to its transaction costs.**

**Justice is a category; degree of justice is an annotation.** Justice = (whether an arrangement lowers interaction friction), judged case by case as a binary (in the First Axiom's sense); degree of justice (the level of TC, comparisons like TC(S) < TC(S')) is an **engineering-layer annotation** (outline precedent: "game solver = efficiency annotation source"). The measure is relative to boundary B (what counts as "one transaction" is redrawn with scale — the boundary is the scale). **Decision rule**: if an institution claims to promote "justice" but actually raises transaction costs, that institution is unjust; the direction of reform is a monotonic decrease of costs — but note the target is a **feasible lower bound, not zero** (see case table, third row).

**Generalization source: the Coase theorem.** When transaction costs are zero, the assignment of property rights is irrelevant (voluntary bargaining always reaches the optimum); when transaction costs are positive, institutional arrangement is decisive. The Fifth Axiom takes its second corollary:

> **Just institutions = institutions that lower transaction costs; just law = law that minimizes friction.**

This elevates it from economics to a general principle of social norms: not only economic institutions — the justness of any social norm (law, morality, custom) depends on its capacity to lower social transaction costs. Coase's property-rights puzzle (public goods are hard to delimit) is handled by the **Boundary theorem**: create operable boundaries for public goods (carbon-emission quotas, etc.) — a thing is determined at its boundary; engineering the boundary is engineering the property right.

**The governance quadruple.** Democracy = collective order; populism = collective permissiveness; autocracy = individual order; dictatorship = individual permissiveness (by definition). The quadruple's axis is **order/permissiveness** (organizational mode), not freedom/servitude (individual state) — the two axes cross, neither reduces to the other. Case-by-case efficiency levels (e.g., the systemic risk of dictatorship) are given by judgment, not admitted into the definitions (for the efficiency reading see note 5).

**The loop with the first four axioms.**

- **To the Zeroth Axiom**: difference produces the need for transactions; transactions produce costs. Identical agents transact nothing, and the justice problem does not arise — difference is where the justice problem starts.
- **To the First Axiom**: incomplete law (loopholes, contradictions) produces uncertainty costs — a complete rule is precisely well-defined property rights (Coase's premise of "clearly delimited property rights"). The First Axiom judges an institution's "correctness"; the Fifth Axiom rates an institution's "goodness": **a correct institution = a complete institution that lowers friction**.
- **To the Third Axiom**: turbulence = the manifestation of high transaction costs ("when gods fight, mortals suffer" is the spillover of conflict costs); institutions are infrastructure for unblocking (credit makes expectations reliable → negotiation/monitoring/enforcement costs fall; prices compress information costs — Sixth Axiom). **C_info is the unblocking cost** — the Games theorem and the justice measurement theorem share the same source.
- **To the Fourth Axiom**: a just institution = correct rules (order) plus retained legitimate openness (freedom), compressing pseudo-openness (permissiveness) — property rights/rules delimit the boundary between "known" and "open"; this is the institutional face of the Fourth Axiom. Note: not "maximize freedom" (freedom is a category and cannot be maximized).

**Pruning theorem: prune pseudo-justice.** What pruning cuts is "non-efficient justice," i.e. **justice that does not take efficiency as its form** — pseudo-justice: claiming justice while actually increasing friction. Isomorphic to "non-ignorant freedom = pseudo-freedom"; it does not cut justice itself. Privilege, monopoly, and status discrimination are typical: increasing friction in the name of justice. **Pruning pseudo-justice = lowering social transaction costs**, in the same direction as unblocking and releasing expression.

**Case table.**

| Object | Verdict | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Pseudo-just institutions (privilege, monopoly, status discrimination) | Claim justice while actually increasing friction; do not take efficiency as their form | Pruning (prune pseudo-justice); engineering layer |
| Interrogator-style institutions (manufacturing permissiveness/blockage) | Manufacturing blockage = manufacturing transaction costs; degree of justice collapses | Judged unjust; unblocking belongs to the engineering layer |
| Zero-transaction-cost ideal (Coase world / utopia) | Idealized boundary (same type as "efficient markets"); zero TC is of the same type as zero freedom (others' intentions fully visible = omniscience) | Judged an idealization, not a realistic design target; the goal is the feasible lower bound |

**Self-reference and scale-freeness.** This axiom refers to itself and applies to itself:

> **Scientific layer**: justice is efficiency (content unchanged, an eternal law of science). **Text layer**: The Principle's own "institutional efficiency" is testable — its criteria, cases, and procedures lower the transaction costs of judgment and coordination: **counterexamples are checkable = low testing cost** (one suffices), commitment/delivery = credit accumulation (First value), the Extraterritorial enforcement theorem = refusal to be drawn into cost-shifting disputes.

Anti-philosophy is anti-DDoS (Q&A verbatim): semantic drift and responsibility-shirking are **cost shifting** — dumping argumentative costs onto the opponent, which is precisely inefficient "justice" (the argumentative form of pseudo-justice). Mutable self-reference is forbidden: efficiency must not be used to redefine "correctness" (the criterion belongs to the First Axiom) — a cheap wrong rule is not correct; "cost reduction" must not be used to endorse pseudo-justice. **The axiom is scale-free**: transaction costs contain no scale parameter — applicable to pairs, organizations, states, inter-species, and human-machine; the expressive space is matter/mind neutral.

**Summary.** The Fifth Axiom does three things:

1. **Operationalizes justice**: moral concept → engineering proposition (lower transaction costs); the criterion of **good = efficiency** lands;
2. **Unifies institutional evaluation**: the justness of constitutions, laws, property rights, markets, and customs reduces to one question — does it lower or raise friction?
3. **Connects judgment and effect**: normative judgment (justice) falls back onto testable institutional effect (transaction costs) — judgment and effect unified.

The naive semantics of "justice" always carried cost words and boundary words — "justice is efficiency" is not imposed but a strictification of intuition (same treatment as "correctness admits no loophole").

### 2. The Coasean ideal is of the same type as the EMH

"With zero transaction costs, institutions don't matter" is an idealized boundary, exactly of the same type as the Third Axiom note 6's "efficient market hypothesis = blockage → 0." And **zero transaction costs = zero freedom** (same type as the Fourth Axiom's "zero turbulence = zero freedom"): others' intentions fully visible is omniscience; bargaining disappears and choice disappears. So the realistic target is the **feasible lower bound**: under the premise that freedom (legitimate openness) exists and blockage cannot be zeroed, compress friction to the feasible minimum. Utopia is judged an idealization, not a design target.

### 3. TC component table (engineering-layer decomposition)

| Component | Content | System counterpart |
|---|---|---|
| Information cost | Search, verification, transmission | Unblocking cost (C_info, same source as the Games theorem) |
| Negotiation cost | Bargaining, contracting | Reducible by credit infrastructure (First value) |
| Monitoring cost | Execution oversight, breach detection | Reducible by price/credit (Sixth Axiom) |
| Enforcement cost | Compulsion, punishment | Engineering layer (police beyond the courthouse) |
| Conflict cost | Wasteful destruction from clashing interests | Turbulence spillover (when gods fight, mortals suffer) |

This is a **measurement decomposition** (engineering layer), not the definitional layer — the definition of justice lies in "lowering friction" (a category); the components are cuts of the measure.

### 4. The philosophical quadrant for contrast

Utilitarianism (greatest happiness), Kantian deontology (universalizable maxims), Rawls (difference principle / original position), Nozick (entitlement justice) are the philosophical quadrant's indeterminate schemes: either no criterion (happiness, the original position cannot be delivered), or no content (deontology gives form but no efficiency rating), or neglect of the cost of delimiting property rights (Nozick). This axiom **replaces** them with efficiency (the certain side) — it does not synthesize: the love-of-wisdom boundary law puts the philosophy/science boundary at certainty. The comparison table is kept as genealogy, not as reconciliation (anti-philosophy is anti-no-commitment-no-delivery).

The case of trolley-problem-style moral-intuition debate: no criterion present, no deliverable test → returned to the philosophical quadrant (no commitment, no delivery); it does not constitute a judgment of justice — a live demonstration of scientific demarcation.

### 5. Efficiency reading of the governance quadruple (inference, not definition)

Democracy (collective order): rule-bound procedures lower the transaction costs of collective decisions — decisions are slow, but the high cost of violent conflict is avoided. Populism (collective permissiveness): rules fail, emotion dominates, costs soar. Autocracy (individual order): fast short-term decisions, concentrated systemic risk, high long-term costs (corruption, information distortion, innovation suppression). Dictatorship (individual permissiveness): no rule constraints — the highest-cost form. Note: **the definitions live in the quadruple grid (collective/individual × order/permissiveness); efficiency levels are case-by-case inferred judgments** and are not admitted into the definitions.

### 6. Credit and price: the two infrastructures of unblocking

The bridge from Third Axiom note 5 completes here: **credit** (predictability → negotiation/monitoring/enforcement costs fall) and **price** (information compression → information costs fall, Sixth Axiom) are the two mainline infrastructures of social friction reduction. The modern form of "even brothers keep clear accounts" is exactly these two: contract (the textualization of credit) and market (the mechanization of price). The engineering content of just institutions = building infrastructure that unblocks and lowers friction.

### 7. Courthouse and DDoS: cost control

The Principle calls itself a courthouse (Q&A: "The Principle is the courthouse, but order beyond the courthouse still requires dispatching many police officers or even armies"). The Extraterritorial enforcement theorem (do not probe deeply into non-existence) and the scientific secularism law (do not punish non-scientific persons) are both **cost-control clauses**: confine adjudication costs to the verdict, do not burn costs on entanglement. Anti-philosophy is anti-DDoS — semantic drift and responsibility-shirking are cost-shifting argumentation (dumping verification costs onto the opponent), precisely the form inefficient "justice" takes in the domain of debate.

### 8. Intra-system inference on distributive justice

Distributive unfairness → turbulence → rising costs (Third Axiom) → falling degree of justice. This is the intra-system chain (not an empirical assertion): the distribution problem is absorbed into the efficiency criterion; no independent postulate of distributive justice is needed. Note that the inference only treats "how distribution affects friction," not "how distribution ought to be" — if the latter exceeds the system's criteria (no deliverable test), it retreats into the philosophical quadrant under the love-of-wisdom boundary law.

### 10. Application and conjecture layers

- **Direction of reform** (application): streamlining approvals, improving the rule of law, protecting property rights, promoting information flow — all concretizations of friction reduction;
- **AGI governance reference** (application/conjecture): designing governance rules for a multi-AGI society aims at minimizing interaction costs — standardized protocols, transparent rules, enforceable contracts;
- **Institutional economics / law and economics** (engineering-layer toolbox): transaction-cost economics (Williamson), property-rights theory (Coase/Demsetz), institutional change (North), public choice (Buchanan), law and economics (liability rules, contract law, constitutional law) — all usable as engineering-layer toolkits for measuring TC and designing institutions.

### 11. Summary

The Fifth Axiom is the system's social criterion: **justice is efficiency; good = efficiency**. It holds of itself (courthouse costs are controlled, counterexamples are checkable), it gives institutions a measure (degree of justice inversely proportional to transaction costs, a category plus engineering-layer annotation), it gives governance a quadruple (the order/permissiveness axis), it gives pseudo-justice a pruning (prune pseudo-justice), and it gives ideals a boundary (zero TC is an idealization, not a target). Judgment and effect unified: justice is no longer debated in the halls but settled in clear books — even brothers keep clear accounts.
