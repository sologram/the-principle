> Philosophy is the study of big questions. However it separates, any discipline separated off that faces big questions will still become philosophical questions. Seen from the angle of big questions: first, no matter how philosophy's branches are separated out, the big questions themselves are never separated out. As Wilson said in *Philosophy in Brief*: philosophy is not a matter of individual concepts, such as alimony, deflation, and perspective, but of universal concepts, such as causality, knowledge, and freedom. A person can do without individual concepts, but cannot live without universal concepts. His work of philosophical principles is composed of seven great ideas (science, spirit, freedom, knowledge, language, objectivity, God). More importantly, what makes a philosophical question a big question is that it can never be solved. So science wants to come and take over philosophy's achievements — let it take them; what is scientific will one day be taken over by science, and what is not scientific belongs to philosophy, no matter when. Therefore Salvador said: "Some questions can receive satisfying answers, and those are the questions science deals with; others, we believe, can never be thoroughly solved. >
> — _Zhang Fa, 2010, Journal of Jianghan University_

## Challenges

|Axiom|Scientific principle of potential friction|What to check|
|-|-|-|
|Existence is difference|Identical particles in quantum statistics|Whether identity conflicts with "existence is difference," or on the contrary confirms it (individuality arises from differences of state)|

|Meaning is price|Theories of meaning in philosophy of language|Whether Frege's sense/reference distinction can be absorbed by "price"|
|Freedom is ignorance|Theories of free will|Compatible with compatibilism; may butt heads with libertarianism|
|Things tend toward completeness|Second law of thermodynamics|"Completeness" is an epistemological concept; must be confirmed that it does not get conflated with entropy increase|
