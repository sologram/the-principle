# Twilight of the Guild

DeepSeek V4

On September 11, 25 Fields Medalists — among them Terence Tao, Yu (Deng Yu), and Peter Scholze — jointly issued an open letter bitterly denouncing *A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics*; less than a month later, on October 6, OpenAI released 722 AI-generated mathematics manuscripts in a single batch on GitHub. 42% of the manuscripts had passed Lean formal verification, 10 disclosed their complete chains of thought, and the rest underwent no peer review whatsoever. Theoretical computer scientist Scott Aaronson described this as the mathematics world's "apocalypse" (Mathocalypse).

The next day, Terence Tao reposted the boycott statement of the Association for Human Mathematics (AHM), quoting its most morally stirring line: "Releasing more than 700 files at once displays not academic research, but power."

This statement is half right. It is indeed a display of power, but the hubris on display belongs not to those releasing the technology — it belongs to the passing of the old power. **What it demonstrates is precisely this: academia no longer possesses the power it fancied it held in monopoly.**

## One

For centuries, the academic community has maintained a quasi-sacred secular authority: it held a monopoly on the right of final judgment over "what counts as valid knowledge."

Such authority derives neither from legal authorization nor from an a priori endowment of truth. It rests on two thoroughly practical monopolies: **the scarcity of verification capability** and **the closure of distribution channels.**

In the long, slow era of handicraft, the guild was the most efficient form of knowledge production. To guarantee the purity and exclusivity of the craft, the guild constructed a sophisticated defense in depth: harsh barriers to entry, long years of apprenticeship, stern peer review, an esoteric jargon system, and an arbitral court built from the top journals and the faculty appointment system. For a derivation to go from draft to an acknowledged "theorem," it had to pass through every secretion and stamping of this entire digestive system.

This system genuinely earned its historical merits, but its supremacy rested on a fundamental premise: **outside this system, no one could independently produce and adjudicate knowledge.**

Yet the vitality of a monopoly depends only on the absence of alternatives. Once alternative verification machines and distribution networks arrive, the guild's institutional barriers decay overnight into self-congratulatory red tape.

## Two

What, exactly, did OpenAI's 722 manuscripts shatter?

They skipped peer review, yet distributed their results directly to a globally public code repository; they never applied to the guild for certification, yet resting on the Lean formal verification engine they completed a machine-closed loop of underlying logic; they even retracted 3 papers within 24 hours of release over a symbol-level flaw, affecting 2 others — in the guild's traditional eyes, such crudeness and haste all but constitute a desecration of academic dignity.

But this wild growth precisely exposes the rupture between the old and new paradigms: **it is imperfect and riddled with impurities, yet its cycle of derivation and self-correction is measured in hours, while the guild's referee reports still take months or even years.**

The guild's defenders always fall back to the last philosophical trench: they insist that proving a problem, punching through a lemma, is not the same as "understanding mathematics" — that machines lack the construction of deep concepts, and still less possess a mathematician's aesthetic and taste.

This defense is airtight in rhetoric, yet pales against evolutionary reality. Every scientific community knows full well: the vast "normal science" that keeps a discipline running day to day is itself built from the cornerstone of countless concrete derivations, local verifications, and technical lemmas. When the construction of the discipline's entire foundation is bypassed by computing power, the priests perched on the top floor, in charge of explaining "taste," naturally lose the legitimacy to collect tolls.

The threshold is not merely sinking; it is being razed flat. An ordinary researcher with no academic pedigree, aided by AI and formal proof tools, can derive and verify a proposition within hours. He need not hand a pledge of loyalty to a famous master, need not wait on a journal's favor — he need only push the formalized code into public space and let the algorithm itself test it for truth or falsity.

The guild is no longer the only gateway to truth, nor even the principal one.

## Three

Confronted with this impact, academia put on the textbook defensive posture: expressing concern, publicly condemning, calling on peers to refuse cooperation.

These are the standard courtesies of an ancient guild, but no longer effective institutional weapons. Academia seems to possess many means of resistance, yet has in fact fallen into a logical predicament in which it is constrained at every step:

* **Declare non-recognition?** If the publicly released manuscripts contain genuine mathematical advances, refusing to recognize them only makes the existing community look blind and parochial;
* **Refuse to participate in verification?** The Lean codebase sits right there; nearly half of the derivations have already been machine-verified without a flaw. The guild's refusal to take it up simply hands the powers of verification and interpretation wholesale to the algorithmic community;
* **Call for a total boycott?** The new generation of young scholars has long since internalized AI in the laboratory as irreversible research infrastructure; a boycott order can only end as the older generation of gatekeepers exiling themselves.

So academia could ultimately settle on nothing but open letters and moral appeals as its main response. This does not spring from some noble prudence, but because under the steamroller of computing power and decentralized verification, **all that remains is this declaratory strategy — the lowest in cost, the smallest in risk, and also the most visibly feeble.**

## Four

When tech companies announce "we are entering a new era of discovery," most people take it for Silicon Valley-style tech PR. To read it merely as "AI discovered new mathematics" is still to stay within a narrow instrumentalist perspective.

The true core of this upheaval is **the transfer and reorganization of the power of knowledge production**:

1. **Compute companies** have taken over the production capacity for ultra-large-scale derivation and formal search;
2. **Formal logic tools** have taken over the absolute arbitration of the correctness of local derivations;
3. **Decentralized open networks** have taken over the real-time distribution of knowledge.

The triune structure of "production, verification, distribution" — tightly bound and fused into a single body by the academic guilds over the past several centuries — is at this moment utterly decoupled.

This is by no means a speculative game about whether silicon-based minds surpass human intelligence; it is a classic living drama of institutional evolution: **the gatekeepers still clutch their rust-pocked keys, but the open country beyond the walls has long been roaring with voices — the existence of the gate itself has ceased to matter.**

## Five

Terence Tao has warned with concern that generation on this scale will produce "indigestion of proofs."

This diagnosis is extraordinarily perceptive, yet its vantage remains confined to the old regime's field of vision. The "humans" he refers to are, subconsciously, still equated with "the academic community composed of professional mathematicians."

The so-called "indigestion" is in essence only **the indigestion of the guild's central stomach sac.** The guild is accustomed to metabolizing every input through a single centralized academic bureaucracy; faced with an exponentially surging flood of information, its review process naturally verges on paralysis.

Yet beyond the guild's high walls, the decentralized explorers do not need to swallow all 722 manuscripts. Each person need only use the tools to understand, extend, and verify the one proposition he himself is interested in. He neither needs to await peers' recognition, nor to prove to any academic authority that he possesses "legitimate qualifications."

This is the true face of the new era: **it is not that machines replace humans in discovering; it is that those who go to discover mathematics no longer need first to become a mathematician ennobled by the guild.**

## Six

The twilight of the guild never means the guild must collapse overnight.

Universities will still grant degrees, top societies will still convene regularly, senior scholars will still solemnly appraise papers on the front pages of journals. It will stand for a long time like a well-preserved medieval old town: bricks and stones in tight array, ceremonies grave and solemn, drawing visitors to pay their respects.

But the flood of knowledge has already flowed around the city.

The gate still stands open, the gatekeeper still watches the entrance vigilantly — it is just that the crowd outside the gate has long since scattered toward the boundless open country.
