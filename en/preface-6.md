# Preface

September 11, 2026: An open letter jointly published by 25 Fields Medalists including Terence Tao, Yu Deng, and Peter Scholze—"A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics."

October 6, 2026: OpenAI released 722 AI-generated mathematical manuscripts all at once on GitHub, the so-called "700 documents" incident. This move was described by theoretical computer scientist Scott Aaronson as the mathematical community's "Mathocalypse."

October 7: Terence Tao forwarded the Association for Human Mathematics (AHM) statement, formally calling for a boycott.

# Twilight of the Guild

On October 6, 2026, OpenAI published 722 mathematical manuscripts on GitHub. 42% had undergone Lean formal verification, 10 disclosed complete chains of thought, and the rest were not peer-reviewed. Less than 24 hours after release, 3 were retracted due to a notation error, collaterally affecting two others.

Terence Tao reposted the Association for Human Mathematics statement: "Publishing over 700 documents all at once is a demonstration not of academic research, but of power."

This sentence is half right. It is indeed a demonstration of power, but the target of the demonstration was mistaken. What OpenAI demonstrated was not power over academia, but that **academia no longer possesses the kind of power it thought it had**.

## 1

For centuries, the mathematical community has maintained a special authority: it decides what counts as mathematical knowledge.

This authority is not granted by law, nor does it exist naturally. It is built upon two foundations: **verification capability** and **dissemination channels**. Academia has peer review, formalization tools, journal networks, and textbook systems. For a proof to become a "mathematical result," it must pass through the digestion and recognition of this system.

This system was once the only option. In an era when information flow was slow and verification tools were scarce, guild-like organization was indeed the most effective form of knowledge production. It had entrance rules, master-apprentice transmission, review procedures, and trade jargon. It monopolized the certification of craftsmanship and the arbitration of disputes.

Yet monopoly has never been eternal. The premise of monopoly is: **without this system, you can do nothing.**

## 2

What did OpenAI do?

It did not join the guild, yet completed partial verification using the Lean formalization tool. It did not go through peer review, yet completed dissemination using Twitter and GitHub. It had no guild authorization, yet made mathematicians feel "scooped."

All the guild can do is issue statements, express concern, and call for non-cooperation. These are guild etiquette, not guild power.

More fatally, the barriers upon which the guild depends for existence are disappearing.

An interested ordinary person, spending three hours with AI assistance, can derive a math problem, verify it, and revise it. They do not need to study for ten years first, do not need to apprentice under a master, do not need to wait for journal reviews. They can display their derivations directly in public space for anyone to inspect.

This was unimaginable five years ago. Now, **the guild is no longer the sole entrance to mathematics, nor even the primary one.**

## 3

Academia can certainly continue to criticize. It can declare non-recognition, refuse to verify, and call on peers not to cooperate.

But it dares not actually execute this.

Not recognize? If there is real substance in those proofs, non-recognition will only make academia appear obstinate. Not verify? The Lean formalization libraries are already sitting there, 42% verified by machine; refusing to verify is tantamount to handing over verification authority to others. Not cooperate? The younger generation within academia is already using AI tools; calling for non-cooperation is tantamount to self-isolation.

So all academia has left is to publicly state its position and criticize. This is the lowest-cost, lowest-risk, but least effective mode of response.

This is not because academia chose this strategy, but because **it has no other strategy left**.

## 4

Altman said: "We are entering a new era of discovery."

If understood as "AI discovered new mathematics," that is still merely a technological narrative. But the deeper meaning is: **the power of knowledge production is shifting from the academic community to compute companies.**

Academia's centuries-long monopoly over the authority to determine "what counts as mathematical knowledge" existed because it commanded two things: verification capability and dissemination channels. Now, neither is exclusively owned by it.

Corporations have compute, the public has curiosity and scale, and AI has speed and scale. The three parties are reorganizing their division of labor. Academia retains traditional advantages, but is no longer the sole proprietor.

This is not a story of "AI being smarter than humans"; this is a story of **the gatekeepers discovering that the gate is no longer in their hands**.

## 5

Yet the true new era is neither in OpenAI's PR releases nor in academia's open letters.

It is in that moment when an ordinary person opens their computer and spends three hours solving a math problem to practice. They do not care who has the right to publish, who should criticize whom, or who recognizes whom. They just want to play.

This change will not stop because someone criticizes someone else, nor will it accelerate because someone recognizes someone else. It is already happening.

The guild can continue to exist, continue reviewing, continue issuing statements. But it increasingly resembles a well-preserved ancient city: people live in it, people visit it, people reminisce about it, but newcomers no longer need to enter through its city gates.

## 6

Terence Tao warned of "proof indigestion." He is right, and he is wrong.

What is right: batch-generated proofs indeed exceed the speed of human comprehension. What is wrong: he takes "humanity" by default to mean the "mathematical community." The real indigestion occurs inside the guild, not outside it.

People outside the guild are using AI assistance to solve problems they find interesting. They do not need to understand 722 manuscripts; they only need to understand their own single problem. They do not need guild recognition; they only need to find it interesting themselves.

This is the true meaning of the "new era of discovery": **not that AI discovered mathematics, but that the people who discover mathematics no longer need to become mathematicians first.**

The twilight of the guild is not that the guild has closed its doors. It is that the city gates remain open, but no one has to enter through them anymore.
