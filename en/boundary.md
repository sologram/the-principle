# Boundary: The Unified Structure from Existence to Order

## 1. The Origin of Boundary

The Zeroth Axiom of the Principle:

> Existence = difference

Any existence first means having a distinction from other existences.

Difference is not mere divergence; it is the structural relationship capable of distinguishing an object from other objects.

And for difference to exist stably, it must form:

> Boundary.

Therefore:

```
Difference → Boundary → Object → Existence
```

A boundary is the manifestation of difference and the prerequisite for an object to exist independently.

Without boundaries, things cannot be distinguished; without distinction, things cannot exist.

---

## 2. Boundary and Completeness

The First Axiom:

> Correctness = completeness

A correct description of an object must contain all necessary information for that object to hold.

Therefore, the core of completeness is:

> Boundary closure.

That is:

- Necessary conditions internal to the object cannot be omitted;
- Influencing conditions external to the object cannot be omitted.

Boundary errors fall mainly into two classes:

### Internal Leakage

There exist undescribed necessary parts internal to the object.

For example:

- A mathematical proof omits conditions;
- A scientific model omits variables;
- An institutional design omits responsibilities.

### External Leakage

The object depends on external conditions, but the boundary does not contain them.

For example:

- Ignoring environmental constraints;
- Ignoring stakeholder subjects;
- Ignoring scope of applicability.

Therefore:

```
Difference generates boundary
Boundary determines object
Completeness closes boundary
Correctness describes object
```

---

## 3. Boundaries at the Natural Scale

In natural systems, boundaries form stable objects:

| System | Boundary Form |
|-|-|
| Particle | Range of interaction |
| Atom | Electronic structure |
| Organism | Cell membrane, individual boundary |
| Ecosystem | Range of energy and material exchange |

Objects across different scales all rely on some form of internal-external distinction.

---

## 4. Boundaries at the Economic Scale

Social subjects likewise require boundaries.

Property rights are boundaries of resources:

> Who owns, who uses, who bears the consequences.

Enterprises are boundaries of organization:

> Which activities are coordinated internally, which are exchanged through the market.

Contracts are boundaries of transaction:

> How rights and obligations of both parties are demarcated.

Therefore economic order is built upon clear boundaries.

```
Resource difference
 ↓
Property rights boundary
 ↓
Trading rules
 ↓
Market price
```

---

## 5. Boundaries at the Political Scale

The state is the boundary structure of a political subject.

State boundaries include:

| Type | Content |
|-|-|
| Territorial boundary | Jurisdictional space |
| Identity boundary | Scope of citizens |
| Legal boundary | Scope of rule application |
| Power boundary | Scope of decision-making authority |

One of the core issues of political order is determining subject boundaries and power boundaries.

---

## 6. Boundaries at the Legal Scale

Statutes are the formalized expression of social boundaries.

Law determines through rules:

- Boundaries of rights;
- Boundaries of obligations;
- Boundaries of prohibitions;
- Boundaries of liabilities.

A complete legal rule needs to clarify:

```
Who the subject is
What the behavior is
What the conditions are
What the consequences are
```

Therefore:

> Law is the encoding system for boundaries of social subjects.

---

## 7. Boundary and Efficiency

The Fourth Axiom:

> Justice = efficiency

Efficiency requires that responsibilities, benefits, and costs correspond to well-defined subjects.

If boundaries are vague:

- Rights and liabilities cannot correspond;
- Information cannot be transmitted;
- Incentives cannot be formed.

Therefore:

```
Clear boundaries
 ↓
Explicit responsibility
 ↓
Effective coordination
 ↓
Efficiency gain
```

---

## 8. Unified Definition of Boundary

From the perspective of the Principle:

> A boundary is the object demarcation formed by difference, and the necessary structure for a system to maintain independence and achieve completeness.

The complete chain:

```
Difference
 ↓
Boundary
 ↓
Object
 ↓
Completeness
 ↓
Correctness
 ↓
Rules
 ↓
Order
 ↓
Efficiency
```

Boundaries run across all scales:

```
Physical boundary
→ Biological boundary
→ Individual boundary
→ Property rights boundary
→ Enterprise boundary
→ State boundary
→ Civilization boundary
```

Therefore, boundary is the core concept connecting the Zeroth Axiom "existence" and the First Axiom "correctness," as well as the common foundation of natural, economic, political, and legal systems.
