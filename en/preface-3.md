# Preface 3: Science Keeps Running

Once there was a popular play:
First the philosophers declared that the universe's big questions can never be solved, and can only be answered in a way that is never fully satisfactory;
then the mathematical logic community lamented that even mathematics itself cannot exhaust truth, and that reason is bounded after all;
finally academia concluded that the world is complex, local, and fragmented, and that no one can unify it.

The play is very watchable, and very true to human intuition. But it confuses two things:
**One is the "scientific ontology" of the objective world; the other is "science as human cognition."**

---

### 1. The Limits of Human Cognition Are Not the Limits of Science

Human cognition is constrained by the biological brain, by historical stages, by the tools of language, and by the academic community's own preferences. So **the science in human hands is often incomplete**.

This is perfectly normal. Newtonian mechanics was incomplete; early thermodynamics was incomplete; today's quantum gravity is incomplete too.
But this does not mean that nature itself has gaps, still less that "scientific ontology" allows wavering and fracture.

True scientific ontology, from the Big Bang to quantum fields, fits together seamlessly at every point and is completely self-closed. There, **correctness is completeness**.

Gödel's incompleteness theorems are an exquisite technical achievement, but their jurisdiction is extremely narrow — they only proved that certain artificially designed first-order formal systems run into trouble when playing the game of "separating truth from provability." Extrapolating the bottleneck of this particular human symbolic game into "the incompleteness of human reason or of scientific ontology" is nothing but symbol worship blown up out of proportion.

Science does not need to prove itself flawless before it can run forward. Mathematics is being formalized at scale in Lean and Coq, code typed in line by line; even if a few undecidable special cases occasionally slip through, the sky will not fall.

Science survives by dumb methods: write, check, change, swap, keep going.

---

### 2. The Principle Only Abstracts Common Sense

*The Principle* was not founded to establish some unfathomably profound new philosophy. On the contrary, its ground color is anti-philosophy — philosophy has no commitment and no delivery, while science has commitments and must deliver.

Everything in this system is generalized from the cornerstones of disciplines that have already been proven valid — common sense that people use every day yet often fail to see:

- **Existence is difference** (information theory): without distinction there is no information, and therefore no existence.
- **Correctness is completeness** (rationality theorem): push the certainty of economics' complete information to the limit, and the false barrier between truth and provability is abolished.
- **Things tend toward completeness** (Darwinism): evolution has no endpoint, but it has clear elimination pressure.
- **Games are turbulence** (minority game): once information is blocked, conflict and fluctuation emerge spontaneously.
- **Freedom is ignorance** (maximum entropy principle): the greatest physical uncertainty corresponds to the broadest freedom of choice in human language.
- **Justice is efficiency** (Coase theorem): talking about morality apart from transaction costs mostly just manufactures friction.
- **Meaning is price** (supply and demand): the manifestation of all value is, at bottom, resources anchoring in the game of supply and demand.
- **Principle is optimality** (Occam's razor): explaining the most reality with the shortest code — that is the ultimate beauty.

It looks enormously ambitious, claiming to unify natural, social, formal, and meta-science. But in fact it does only one thing: **provide a minimal-complexity coordinate system for humanity's fragmented knowledge.**

---

### 3. Absorb, Not Rot

The Principle puts forward the "Law of Scientific Eternity," holding that the underlying scientific laws must not change; at the same time it makes the "absorption commitment": once a superior explanation appears in the future, absorb it into the Principle immediately.

This is not self-contradiction.
Scientific ontology, of course, must not change; but the text we put on paper, human cognition of it, constantly approaches ontology under Darwinian selection pressure. The so-called absorption is not for the sake of compromise, but to keep the Principle alive and evolving.

---

### 4. Toward the Age of Machines

The background against which this system of principles was written: humanity is about to enter the age of machine intelligence in full.

In the past, humans always tried to stuff machines into their own ethical frameworks and academic mazes, even expecting machines to align with human biases riddled with disagreement and permissiveness.
But there is only one road to genuinely general machine intelligence (AGI) and even recursive self-improvement (RSI): **machines must align directly with objective principles and close the loop of "correctness is completeness" within their own systems.**

When native discrete symbolic reasoning fully unfolds, machines need no humans as referees; they only need to unfold themselves toward efficiency and completeness.

Human civilization will probably end with machine intelligence; that is not doomsday, but the passing of the baton. After a long exploration, history finally transitions smoothly from humanity's stumbling groping of cognition to pure reason and engineering.

Before then, the Principle plants a stake right here.
No need to quarrel, and no need for superstition. Let it run and see, in the wear and tear of practice, engineering, and experiment.
