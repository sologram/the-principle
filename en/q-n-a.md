# Q&A

This document compiles frequently asked questions about the Principle and their answers.

## Basic Questions

### Is the Principle science?

**Yes.**

The criteria for science are: it does not depend on external principles, and it is not internally self-contradictory. The Principle satisfies both conditions.

More specifically:

- The five Laws of the Principle do not depend on any external axioms or assumptions
- The five Laws do not contradict each other, and can be derived from one another
- Each Law can be verified (by searching for counterexamples)
- Each Law does not conflict with any known scientific principle. On the contrary, the Laws have extensive connections with other scientific principles

Unlike religion or philosophy, the Principle explicitly gives the conditions for verification. For example, if an existence that is "not information" can be found, the Zeroth Law is refuted. If a thing that is "incomplete but correct" can be found, the First Law is refuted.

### What is the status of humans in the Principle?

**Humans are processors of information, and are one of the objects to which the Principle applies.**

According to the Zeroth Law, humans are also information. According to the Second Law, humans tend toward completeness — this manifests as various human motivations: survival, seeking knowledge, reproduction, cooperation, and so on.

The particularity of humans in the Principle lies in:

- Humans are the species currently known to have the strongest mind, capable of reflecting on themselves and the world
- Humans have free will (according to the Third Law, freedom is ignorance; human decision-making is too rich for observers, and not easy to predict)
- Humans pursue efficiency (according to the Second Law), and have created high-efficiency cooperation mechanisms such as civilization, law, and markets

Humans are not the "center" of the Principle, but an "embodiment" of the Principle. All human behavior conforms to the Principle, without exception.

The application domains of the Principle include:

**1. Scientific theory construction**
- Provides criteria for theory evaluation: the completeness test
- Guides the axiomatic method: deriving theorems from fundamental principles
- Cross-disciplinary unification: using the same framework to understand physical, biological, and social phenomena

**2. Social institution design**
- Law: pursuing completeness, without loopholes
- Democracy: dispersing decision-making risk, improving system stability
- Markets: the most efficient mechanism for resource allocation

**3. Personal decision-making**
- Avoiding mistakes: checking whether information is complete
- Improving efficiency: achieving goals with minimal resources

**4. AI and machine intelligence**
- Provides a framework of prior knowledge
- Guides the construction of world models
- Defines the criteria for "correct reasoning"

## Theoretical Foundations

### God's existence can explain everything, so why is it not science?

**Because the proposition "God exists" is incomplete.**

The problems with "God exists" are:

- It does not define what "God" is — omniscient and omnipotent? Benevolent? Having a personality? The definition is vague
- It provides no method of verification — existence cannot be proven, and non-existence cannot be proven either
- It can explain everything — which means it provides no predictable information

According to the First Law, complete things are certain. "God exists" is neither certainly existent nor certainly non-existent, so it is not a scientific proposition.

### The Principle can also explain everything, so why is it science?

**Because the propositions of the Principle are complete.**

Unlike "God exists," each Law of the Principle has a clear definition and conditions for verification:

- The Zeroth Law: if an existence that is "not information" is found, the Law is refuted
- The First Law: if a thing that is "incomplete but correct" is found, the Law is refuted
- The Second Law: if a thing that "does not tend toward completeness" is found, the Law is refuted
- The Fourth Law: if a system that is "completely known yet free" is found, the Law is refuted
- The Fifth Law: if a thing with "positive cost yet eternal" is found, the Law is refuted

Falsifiability is a core feature of science. The Principle explicitly gives the conditions for falsification, therefore it is science.

### Is mathematics science?

**Yes. Moreover, mathematics is the highest science.**

Mathematics satisfies all the criteria of science:
- It does not depend on external principles: mathematical axioms are self-evident
- It is not internally self-contradictory: mathematical theorems do not contradict each other
- It is verifiable: mathematical proofs can be checked

The particularity of mathematics lies in:
- Mathematics is a priori — it does not depend on experience
- Mathematics is complete — every proposition is either true or false (under given axioms)
- Mathematics is the foundation of other sciences — physics, economics, etc. all use mathematics to describe laws

Note that the Principle also possesses the above features of mathematics.

### What exactly is calculus?

**Calculus is the mathematical formulation of the First Law.**

The First Law: correctness is completeness. Completeness means "no omission." The core idea of calculus is: decompose continuous change into infinitely small discrete parts, ensuring nothing is omitted.

Integration is "completization of summation" — adding up infinitely many infinitely small parts to obtain the complete total.
Differentiation is "completization of change" — precisely describing the rate of change at a given instant, without omitting any tiny change.

The reason calculus works is precisely that it follows the First Law: through completization, it eliminates uncertainty.

### How to distinguish pseudoscience

Science is a complete doctrine; its most important external necessary demarcation criterion is invariance. Semantic drift under pressure is the most common feature of pseudoscience.

## Philosophical Questions

### Why is there "existence" everywhere but no absolute nothingness?

**This is exactly the content of the Zeroth Law.**

The Zeroth Law: Existence is information.

"Absolute nothingness" means "no information whatsoever." But "no information whatsoever" is itself a piece of information — it describes a state. Therefore, absolute nothingness cannot exist, because once it is described, it is no longer nothingness.

In other words: nothingness is a self-contradictory concept. Saying "nothingness exists" is contradictory, because existence implies having information, while nothingness implies having no information.

### Why does the world exist (or: why do all things exist)?

**According to the First Law and the Second Law: all things exist for (tending toward) completeness.**

Existence is information. Information needs a carrier. A carrier needs stability. Stability needs completeness.

According to the Second Law, incomplete things will eventually perish. Therefore, things that can continue to exist must tend toward completeness. The world exists because it tends toward completeness — black holes and heat death are its ultimate manifestation.

### Do we have free will?

**Yes. Free will is "the ability to make decisions and take actions where others are ignorant of the causes."**

According to the Third Law, freedom is ignorance. When the causes of a person's decision are not fully known to outsiders, that decision is free to outsiders.

Free will is not "decision-making without causes," but decision-making whose "causes cannot be completely predicted." Your decision may be certain to yourself (if you fully understand yourself), but it is unpredictable to other people — that is free will.

### Will there be an afterlife after death?

**If consensus cannot be reached on the existence of an afterlife, then according to the First Law and its corollary, there is no afterlife after death.**

The First Law: complete things are certain. Regarding the "afterlife," there is currently no complete information — no verifiable evidence, no predictable mechanism, no repeatable observations.

Therefore, the proposition "there is an afterlife after death" is incomplete, and cannot be judged to be true.

### How to solve the trolley problem?

**The trolley problem does not exist — this proposition is incomplete.**

The classic form of the trolley problem is: a trolley is about to kill five people; you can pull a switch to divert the trolley onto another track, but that will kill one person. What should you do?

This problem is incomplete, because:
- It lacks information about your relationship with these people
- It lacks background information about these people
- It lacks information about the cost of pulling the switch
- It lacks information about subsequent effects

According to the First Law, any correct judgment requires complete information. The trolley problem deliberately hides key information to manufacture a moral dilemma. This is not a real problem, but a pseudo-problem with incomplete information.

### When "truth," "goodness," and "beauty" conflict, how should we choose?

**We should decisively prioritize "beauty."**

Truth = correctness = completeness
Goodness = cooperation = efficiency (at the cooperation level)
Beauty = efficiency (at the competition level)

Beauty is the direct manifestation of efficiency. When the three conflict:
- Choosing "beauty" means choosing the most efficient path
- A more efficient choice is, in the long run, more likely to approach "truth" and "goodness"

In fact, situations where the three truly conflict are rare. The usual conflict is: short-term interest vs. long-term interest, or local efficiency vs. overall efficiency. Choosing "beauty" (efficiency) can resolve these conflicts uniformly.

**Because there is no correctness in philosophy.**

The real progress in today's social humanities — evolutionary psychology, evolutionary economics, cultural evolution theory — is all based on Darwinism. "Social Darwinism was abandoned" is a political-correctness myth, not an academic fact.

## Social Questions

### How should we build our society?

**Goal: justice. Principles (values): in order from high to low — "credit - honesty - order - freedom - democracy."**

Justice is efficiency — maintaining the operation of society at the lowest cost. To achieve justice, the following values must be arranged by priority:

1. **Credit**: commitments must be fulfilled, otherwise cooperation costs rise
2. **Honesty**: information is truthful, reducing verification costs
3. **Order**: rules are clear, behavior is predictable
4. **Freedom**: reducing unnecessary constraints, improving efficiency
5. **Democracy**: dispersing decision-making risk, improving system stability

These values sometimes conflict. Handle them by priority: credit > honesty > order > freedom > democracy.

### How should we design a country's political system?

**Under the above goal and principles, the method is: federal autonomy based on a representative system.**

Representative system: citizens elect representatives, and the representatives are responsible for decision-making. It is more efficient than direct democracy.
Federal autonomy: local autonomy predominates, with central coordination as secondary. It adapts to differences across regions, improving overall efficiency.

The advantages of this system:
- High decision-making efficiency (representative system is more efficient than direct democracy)
- Strong adaptability (federalism permits local differences)
- Good stability (power is dispersed, tyranny is difficult)

### How should we make laws to adapt to social development and protect everyone's rights?

**Common law based on legal equality and freedom of migration.**

The core features of common law:
- Judge-made law: law accumulates through precedents
- Strong adaptability: new situations can create new precedents
- Evolutionary: law evolves as society evolves

Legal equality: treating identical situations identically, reducing uncertainty.

Freedom of migration: individuals can vote with their feet, forcing local governments to improve governance.

This system satisfies the efficiency principle: adapting to change at the lowest cost.

### What is moral ethics?

**Morality is a sign and supplement of an imperfect legal system.**

When the law cannot cover all social behavior, morality fills the gaps.

An ideal society does not need morality, only law. Because:
- Law is clear and enforceable
- Morality is vague and hard to enforce

The reason morality exists is that the law is imperfect. As the law gradually becomes complete, the role of morality will gradually weaken. The best moral system is: no morality, all law.

### What is happiness?

**Happiness is the state of desire being satisfied.**

Desire originates from perceived incompleteness. When incompleteness is eliminated and desire satisfied, happiness arises.

Happiness is temporary, because new incompleteness will continually arise. Pursuing eternal happiness is pursuing eternal completeness — this is the ultimate goal of the Second Law, and a goal that cannot be fully achieved.

### What is beauty?

**Beauty is efficiency, especially efficiency related to sexual competition.**

The essence of beauty is competitiveness. The features of beauty — symmetry, harmony, health, intelligence — are all signals of high efficiency:
- Symmetry indicates normal development, no disease
- Harmony indicates body-mind coordination
- Health indicates strong viability
- Intelligence indicates high information-processing efficiency

Beauty is not subjective, but has objective criteria: what is efficient is beautiful. Different cultures have somewhat different concrete standards of beauty, but the core is always efficiency.

### What mistakes do people often make when recognizing and understanding things? How can we try our best to avoid wrong judgments and decisions?

**All mistakes originate from violating the First Law. The general method to avoid mistakes is to master the Principle.**

Common types of mistakes:
1. **Incomplete information**: drawing conclusions without gathering sufficient information
2. **Ignoring boundaries**: infinitely extending a theory of limited applicability
3. **Self-contradiction**: simultaneously holding mutually contradictory beliefs
4. **Confusing correlation with causation**: taking statistical correlation as causal relation

Methods to avoid mistakes:
- Before deciding, check whether the information is complete
- Clarify the applicable boundaries of a theory
- Check whether beliefs contradict each other
- Distinguish correlation from causation

### Why does a society that already has obvious overcapacity still have widespread poverty?

**Because the foundation of natural science has already been locked into the Principle, while "social science" has unfortunately been locked into philosophy; these are observable facts.**

### What is the root of discrimination?

**The root of discrimination is incomplete information — using easily obtained superficial information as a substitute for the real basis of judgment.**

Discrimination is differential treatment of individuals or groups based on irrelevant information. For example:
- Racial discrimination: judging ability by skin color
- Gender discrimination: judging suitable work by gender
- Age discrimination: judging learning ability by age

The reason discriminators do this is: the cost of obtaining real information is too high, while the cost of obtaining superficial information (skin color, gender, age) is almost zero.

Methods to reduce discrimination:
- Pay the cost of obtaining real information
- Increase the cost of discrimination (e.g., anti-discrimination laws)

## Science and Method

### When we ask why, what are we asking?

**We are asking "what is the cause," that is, searching for causal connections between things.**

"Why" has three levels:
1. **Proximate cause**: the direct cause (Why is it dark? Because the sun has set)
2. **Remote cause**: the fundamental cause (Why does the sun set? Because the Earth rotates)
3. **Ultimate cause**: the terminal cause (Why does the Earth rotate? Because of conservation of angular momentum, originating from the formation of the solar system)

According to the Principle, all "whys" can ultimately be traced back to the five Laws. When an answer is consistent with the Laws, it is the final answer.

### How does economics solve the problem that people are not completely rational?

**Economics does not need to solve this problem, because the "rational man" is an approximation of behavior, not a description of psychology.**

The rational man assumption: people achieve their goals with the highest efficiency.

Critics say: people are not completely rational; people have emotions, biases, short-sightedness.

Response:
- The rational man assumption describes **behavioral outcomes**, not **psychological processes**
- In market competition, irrational behavior is eliminated (Second Law)
- In the long run, human behavior tends toward rationality

The method of economics is: assume people are rational, predict behavior, test with data. If prediction fails, revise the assumption. This is the scientific method.

### Can the concept of "infinity" really be understood by humans?

**No. Because infinity implies incompleteness, which contradicts the First Law.**

According to the corollary of the First Law, all correct things have boundaries, and are finite. The concept of infinity:
- Cannot be completely imagined
- Cannot be completely described
- Cannot be completely manipulated (such as computed)

The "infinity" in mathematics is a formal concept, defined through finite rules. For example, an "infinite set" is a definition, not an object that can be completely enumerated. What we understand is "the rules of infinity," not "infinity itself."

### What problems does deep-network-based AI have?

**The problems lie in prior knowledge and valuation.**

The advantages of deep networks: they can learn complex mapping relations from data.
The disadvantages of deep networks:
1. **Lack of prior knowledge**: requires massive data, unlike humans who can learn from few samples
2. **Difficulty of valuation**: no explicit value function, does not know what is right
3. **Uninterpretability**: the interior is a black box; it is hard to understand why a decision was made

The Principle can provide solutions:
- Prior knowledge: the five Laws are the foundation of all intelligent systems
- Valuation criteria: completeness and efficiency can serve as a universal value function
- Interpretability: reasoning processes based on the Principle are transparent

## Other Questions

### Why does everyone think they are the most unique?

**Because everyone's information is incomplete — they know much about themselves and little about others.**

According to the Third Law, ignorance is freedom. Each person's cognition of themselves is "firsthand," while their cognition of others is "observational."
- Toward oneself: knowing the complexity, contradictoriness, and changeability of one's inner world
- Toward others: only able to see surface behavior, unable to see the inner world

Therefore, everyone feels their own inner world is richer and more unique than others'. This is a cognitive bias, originating from information asymmetry.

### Can civilization be seen as part of nature?

**Yes. Civilization is part of nature, and follows natural laws.**

The opposition between "nature" and "artificial" is false. According to the Zeroth Law, civilization is also information, and is also existence. According to the Second Law, the evolution of civilization also tends toward completeness.

The essence of civilization is a high-efficiency mode of social organization. Humans improved efficiency through cooperation, which fully conforms to natural law — other species also cooperate (such as ant colonies and wolf packs), just at lower efficiency than humans.

### How to understand the relationship between right and good?

**Right is completeness, good is efficiency. Right is the premise, good is the goal.**

- **Right**: no omission, no contradiction, clear boundaries
- **Good**: achieving goals with minimal resources

The relationship:
- Things that are not right cannot be truly good — because errors cause efficiency loss
- Things that are right are not necessarily good — they may be inefficient, or in the wrong direction
- The ideal state is: both right and good — complete and efficient

### How to refute determinism?

**There is no need to refute it. Determinism is correct, but it does not mean there is no free will.**

Determinism: all events are determined by prior causes.
Free will: human decision-making cannot be completely predicted by observers.

The two do not contradict. According to the Third Law, freedom is ignorance. Even if your decision is, in principle, determined by prior causes, as long as those causes are not known to outsiders, your decision is free.

The real question is not "does determinism hold," but "is prediction possible." Due to incomplete information, complete prediction of complex systems (such as the human brain) is impossible — that is the actual source of freedom.

### How should education change to meet different children's needs and cultivate qualified people?

**Education should be personalized and efficiency-oriented.**

Problems with current education:
- Standardization: using the same curriculum to teach all students
- Low efficiency: large amounts of time spent on repetition and test preparation
- Information asymmetry: students do not know their own interests and abilities

Directions for improvement:
1. **Personalization**: customizing curricula according to students' interests and abilities
2. **Efficiency orientation**: mastering core knowledge in the shortest time, leaving more time for exploration
3. **Information transparency**: helping students understand their own strengths and weaknesses

The goal of education is: let each person find the position of their highest efficiency.

### How do people's aggressive behaviors arise? How can war be avoided?

**Aggressive behavior originates from resource competition and incomplete information. The method to avoid war is to improve cooperation efficiency.**

Causes of aggressive behavior:
1. **Resource scarcity**: competition is unavoidable
2. **Information asymmetry**: not trusting the other side, striking first for advantage
3. **Zero-sum thinking**: believing one side's gain equals the other side's loss

Methods to avoid war:
1. **Improve resource efficiency**: reduce scarcity
2. **Increase information transparency**: reduce misjudgment
3. **Establish cooperation mechanisms**: transform zero-sum games into positive-sum games

War is the least efficient mode of competition. As civilization progresses and cooperation efficiency increases, war will gradually decrease.

### Why do people commit suicide? How can suicide be avoided?

**Suicide is an individual's pursuit of their own completeness; the individual's right to dispose of their own life should be respected.**

Causes of suicide:
1. **Suffering exceeds what one can bear**: life is no longer efficient
2. **Loss of value**: unable to find a meaning of existence
3. **Pursuit of completeness**: ending a state of uncertainty through death

Methods to avoid suicide:
1. **Reduce suffering**: medical care, psychological support
2. **Create value**: help find a meaning of existence
3. **Provide choices**: euthanasia makes death a controllable choice, rather than a sudden act of despair

Society should respect the individual's right to dispose of their own life, and provide convenience and assistance for suicide (euthanasia). This sounds counterintuitive, but it can actually reduce impulsive suicides — when death becomes a controllable choice, people treat life more rationally.

### How do social classes arise? What impact do they have on social development?

**Classes are the result of unequal resource distribution, and have both positive and negative impacts on social development.**

Causes of class formation:
1. **Efficiency differences**: different people have different efficiencies, leading to differences in resource accumulation
2. **Information asymmetry**: high-efficiency people hold more information and resources
3. **Institutional solidification**: vested interests design institutions to protect themselves

Impacts of classes:
- **Positive**: incentivize competition, promote efficiency gains
- **Negative**: resource monopoly, hindering social mobility

According to the Second Law, classes are the result of evolution, not artificial design. But overly solidified classes reduce social efficiency, and are eventually broken (by revolution or reform).
